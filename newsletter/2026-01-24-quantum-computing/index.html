This comprehensive review covers 165 articles published in January 2026, surveying a field undergoing a profound shift. Federal agencies have declared 2026 the “Year of Quantum Security,” the Ethereum Foundation has formed a dedicated post-quantum security team with $2 million in research prizes, and a Jefferies strategist has removed Bitcoin from his model portfolio over quantum threats. Meanwhile, error correction researchers achieve 10,000x reductions in error rates, neutral-atom systems scale toward 100,000-qubit architectures, and the University of Waterloo launches the world’s first open-source full-stack quantum computer. Quantum computing is graduating from laboratory curiosity to infrastructure that must be secured, scaled, and democratized.

The Security Awakening¶

2026 has been designated the “Year of Quantum Security” by the FBI and NIST, marking a coordinated shift from theoretical concerns to operational mandates across federal agencies source . A launch event in Washington, D.C. on January 12 brought together federal agencies including the White House, DHS, and CISA to address what officials call the “harvest now, decrypt later” threat—where adversaries steal encrypted data today intending to decrypt it once quantum computers mature source .

The urgency is translating into action. The Ethereum Foundation formed a dedicated Post-Quantum security team and announced the $1 million Poseidon Prize to strengthen the network’s quantum-resistant hash function. Combined with last year’s $1 million Proximity Prize for broader post-quantum research, the foundation has committed $2 million to blockchain defense source . Vitalik Buterin, Ethereum’s co-founder, estimates a 20% probability that quantum computers break current cryptography before 2030.

The financial sector is responding. Christopher Wood, Global Head of Equity Strategy at Jefferies, removed the 10% Bitcoin allocation from his recommended portfolio, replacing it with gold, citing concerns that quantum computing could crack cryptocurrency encryption source . Coinbase formed an Independent Advisory Board on Quantum Computing and Blockchain , bringing in experts including Dan Boneh of Stanford and Scott Aaronson to assess risks source . The board’s first position paper is expected early next year source .

Yet awareness has not translated into preparedness. 56% of mid-sized organizations admit they are not prepared for quantum threats source . Research from Bain & Company finds that 90% of companies lack systems to defend against quantum security threats, while 71% expect attacks within five years source . Only 10% have a roadmap to address quantum risks source .

Bitcoin developers maintain that threats are years away, noting slow progress in quantum computing and ongoing work on post-quantum cryptography source . But the divergence between enterprise anxiety and enterprise preparation is stark. As one analyst put it, companies focused narrowly on AI risk missing the quantum shift source .

The Security Paradox¶

Even as organizations mobilize against quantum threats to encryption, researchers have exposed a irony: quantum computers themselves have serious security flaws. Researchers at Penn State identified serious security weaknesses in current quantum computing systems, including vulnerabilities in program verification, circuit design, and hardware crosstalk source .

The research, funded by the U.S. National Science Foundation and Intel, found that current security methods for classical computers are inadequate for quantum systems source . Swaroop Ghosh, professor of computer science and electrical engineering at Penn State, warns that as quantum computers become more integrated into industry, these flaws could expose sensitive corporate and personal information to theft and tampering source .

The vulnerabilities span multiple layers. Circuit topology issues could enable information encoding tampering, while hardware crosstalk between adjacent qubits could facilitate information leakage source . The researchers argue that protecting quantum computers requires securing both software and physical hardware from the ground up—a multi-layered defense strategy that current systems largely lack source .

This creates a paradox: quantum computing is viewed as an offensive threat to existing encryption, yet quantum systems themselves are vulnerable to attack. As industry adoption grows, addressing these foundational security challenges will be essential before quantum computers can be safely deployed for sensitive commercial and research applications source .

Error Correction Breakthroughs¶

The technical community is actively addressing quantum computing’s central challenge: error correction. Several advances this month demonstrate real progress toward fault-tolerant systems.

Cat Qubit Advances¶

Alice & Bob announced “Elevator Codes” , a new approach to error correction for cat qubit quantum computers source . The technique uses code concatenation to achieve a 10,000 times lower logical error rate compared to current error correction methods, requiring only 3 times more qubits source .

Diego Ruiz, theoretical physicist at Alice & Bob, notes that this reduction could enable complex molecular simulations sooner than expected source . The company, which has raised €130 million and hired 150 employees, claims its approach reduces hardware requirements for a large-scale quantum computer by 200 times source .

AI-Assisted Decoding¶

EdenCode emerged from stealth with $1.3 million in pre-seed funding to develop a real-time AI decoder for quantum error correction source . The San Jose-based startup, founded by Dr. Wanda Hou (CEO) and Prof. Yi-Zhuang You of UC San Diego, claims its hardware-agnostic neural network achieves a 99.9% error-detection rate and operates up to 10 times faster than conventional methods source .

The technology aims to reduce error rates by a factor of 10 and processes faults in less than a millisecond source . This addresses a critical bottleneck: as quantum systems scale, decoding delays can increase, but EdenCode’s approach is designed to scale without increasing processing time source .

Research & Tools¶

The snaQCs2025 project, a German consortium including neQxt GmbH, Fraunhofer Institute for Applied Solid State Physics IAF, and Point 8 GmbH, launched with €2.5 million in government funding to advance quantum error correction with logical qubits source . The three-year project aims to create a complete development pipeline from simulation to hardware integration source .

Nature published a demonstration of low-overhead quantum error correction codes, representing peer-reviewed validation of progress in the field source .

Microsoft released an updated, open-source Quantum Development Kit focused on creating fault-tolerant quantum applications source . The release includes specialized toolkits for chemistry and error correction, with the full error correction suite expected by late 2026 source . The kit integrates Visual Studio Code and GitHub Copilot to simplify quantum programming source .

Scaling Up: Hardware Advances¶

Neutral-Atom Scaling¶

Neutral-atom quantum computing emerged as a leading platform for scaling this month. Researchers at Columbia University demonstrated a new method combining optical tweezers with metasurfaces, successfully trapping 1,000 strontium atoms and creating a metasurface capable of generating 360,000 optical tweezers source . This lays groundwork for quantum computers with over 100,000 qubits source .

The approach overcomes bulk and cost limitations of previous technologies like spatial light modulators source . For comparison, a Caltech team achieved 6,100 trapped atoms using previous methods source . The Columbia team’s metasurface uses 200 nm pixels—smaller than the wavelength of light—and tolerates optical intensities up to 2,000 W/mm² source . The research was published in Nature source .

Moscow State University and Rosatom successfully tested a 72-qubit neutral-atom quantum computer prototype , utilizing rubidium atoms in optical tweezers with a novel three-zone architecture source . The system achieved 94% two-qubit gate accuracy, fulfilling a 2024 government mandate to scale neutral-atom platforms beyond 50 qubits source . Russia is targeting fault-tolerant quantum systems by 2030 source .

Silicon Progress¶

Researchers in Australia demonstrated an 11-qubit quantum processor using phosphorus atoms in isotopically-pure silicon-28, achieving gate fidelities above 99% source . The system operates at 16 mK and successfully created entangled states of up to eight atoms without performance degradation as qubits were added source .

This silicon-based approach shows promise for easier scaling than superconducting or ion-trap qubits, as it leverages existing semiconductor manufacturing expertise source . The work was published in Nature in 2025 source .

Commercial Deployments¶

Rigetti Computing received an $8.4 million order to deliver a 108-qubit quantum computer to India’s Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC) source . The system, featuring Rigetti’s chiplet-based architecture, is scheduled for on-premises deployment in Bengaluru in the second half of 2026 source . This builds on a memorandum of understanding signed in September 2025 focused on hybrid quantum-classical computing systems source .

Rigetti’s superconducting systems operate with gate speeds of 50-70ns, which the company notes is approximately 1,000 times faster than ion traps and neutral atoms source . The company has previously sold systems with up to 180 qubits source .

The deployment is part of India’s broader quantum initiatives. The country’s National Quantum Mission has a budget of approximately $730 million source . Domestic developments include QpiAI’s 25-qubit Indus system, with a 64-qubit Kaveri processor planned source .

Capital Flows to Quantum¶

Equal1, an Irish quantum semiconductor company, raised $60 million (€51 million) in a funding round led by the Ireland Strategic Investment Fund source . The company’s Bell-1 QPU uses silicon spin qubits built on standard silicon, enabling integration with existing data centers source . The funds will support deployment in European HPC centers and acceleration of the roadmap toward millions of on-chip qubits source . A key deployment is planned for the European Space Agency’s High Performance Compute Centre source . Equal1’s CMOS-manufactured hybrid quantum-classical silicon-on-chip (QSoC) architecture aims to reduce cost and infrastructure barriers source .

Photonic, a Canadian quantum computing company, secured $130 million ($180M CAD) in funding led by Planet First Partners, bringing total capital raised to $271 million ($375M CAD) source . The company is developing fault-tolerant systems combining silicon-based qubits with photonic connectivity using its “Entanglement First Architecture” source . The funding will advance product milestones, expand teams, and deepen customer engagements source .

Microsoft launched the 2026 Quantum Research Pioneers Program (QuPP) , inviting academic researchers to submit proposals for funding up to $200,000 to advance measurement-based approaches for topological quantum computing source . The program focuses on challenges including simulation methods, error correction, and control techniques, with funding decisions announced March 15 and the program beginning August 1, 2026 source .

Democratizing Quantum Access¶

The University of Waterloo launched Open Quantum Design (OQD) , the world’s first open-source, full-stack quantum computer using ion-trapping technology source . OQD provides open access to quantum hardware and software, partnering with Haiqu, Unitary Foundation, and Xanadu to accelerate quantum computing development source . The initiative has 30 software contributors and builds on 8 years of hardware development at the Institute for Quantum Computing (IQC) source .

Co-founders Crystal Senko, Rajibul Islam, and Roger Melko aim to promote collaboration by providing real hardware access for algorithm testing source . This open-source model could accelerate quantum computing development by enabling broader collaboration source .

Sri Lanka inaugurated its first quantum computer , a nuclear magnetic resonance-powered system from Spinqiu housed at the University of Colombo source . The facility will enable hands-on learning and advanced research in quantum mechanics, positioning the country on the global quantum innovation map source .

Colorado School of Mines launched the first undergraduate Bachelor of Science in Quantum Systems Engineering in the United States source . The program addresses the quantum workforce gap through an interdisciplinary curriculum and industry collaboration, including a year-long, industry-sponsored capstone project and access to the Quantum Commons hub source . The first students will enroll Fall 2026, with inaugural graduates expected Spring 2029 source . The program is supported by the Elevate Quantum consortium, which includes 120 organizations source .

The Enterprise Readiness Gap¶

An IBM study of 2,000 executives across 33 countries and 23 industries reveals a striking disconnect: 59% believe quantum-enabled AI will transform their industry by 2030, but only 27% expect their organizations to be using quantum computing by then source . This 32-percentage-point gap indicates significant underpreparation source .

The study finds that 79% of respondents say AI will significantly contribute to revenue by 2030, but companies focused narrowly on AI risk missing the quantum shift source . Quantum computing will integrate with classical computing as complementary tools in hybrid workflows source .

Early adopters are already experimenting. Moderna uses 80-qubit systems to model mRNA secondary structures, while larger optimization problems use 156-qubit systems with 950 non-local gates source . HSBC is exploring quantum for financial optimization source .

The quantum computing market is projected to reach $72 billion by 2035 (high-end projection) or $28 billion (low-end) source . McKinsey estimates quantum computing could unlock $100 billion in value by 2035 source .

Quandela, a photonic quantum computing company, identified four key trends for 2026 : hybrid quantum-classical computing, industrial use cases, error correction, and cybersecurity source . These trends indicate quantum computing is moving from research laboratories to practical adoption source . In Canada, the quantum sector is projected to contribute $17.7 billion to GDP and generate 157,000 jobs by 2045 source .

Global Quantum Alliances¶

International partnerships accelerated this month. Singapore and Japan signed a Memorandum of Cooperation on quantum technology, establishing a bilateral framework with 8 priority areas source . Concurrently, Japanese hardware startup Yaqumo Inc. and Singapore-based Entropica Labs signed an MOU to collaborate on hardware-software co-design for fault-tolerant quantum computers using cold-atom systems source .

The European Photonics for Quantum (P4Q) pilot will launch in 2026 with €50 million in funding, involving 29 partners across 12 countries source . The project focuses on silicon nitride and thin-film lithium niobate photonic chips, aiming to improve reliability and scalability for quantum applications source .

PsiQuantum and Airbus announced a partnership to develop fault-tolerant quantum algorithms for aerospace applications, focusing on computational fluid dynamics source . They co-authored a technical paper presenting a Quantum Lattice Boltzmann Algorithm validated for aircraft aerodynamics, utilizing PsiQuantum’s Construct software suite source .

Quobly and TNO partnered to optimize silicon spin qubit manufacturing , focusing on bridging device engineering and materials science to improve yield and performance source .

Horizon Quantum Computing and Alice & Bob announced a strategic collaboration to accelerate fault-tolerant quantum computing software development source . The partnership integrates Alice & Bob’s quantum emulators with Horizon’s Triple Alpha development platform source .

IQM Quantum Computers and Bechtle will install a five-qubit Spark quantum computer at Heilbronn University of Applied Sciences in Germany later this year source . Bechtle will provide operational support and maintenance for the first three years source .

Ohio Representative Heidi Workman introduced House Bill 650 to establish the Frontier Technologies and Quantum Computing Commission source . The bipartisan commission will study emerging technologies including AI, quantum computing, and biotechnology to inform policy and position Ohio as a leader in innovation source .

The European Quantum Act, set for adoption in 2026, aims to align investment, governance, security, and deployment of quantum technologies to strengthen Europe’s sovereignty and competitiveness source . The policy initiative seeks to bridge gaps in patents and private capital while integrating cryptographic protections like post-quantum cryptography source .

Looking Ahead¶

The developments of January 2026 reveal quantum computing at a crossroads. The security awakening—marked by the “Year of Quantum Security” designation, Ethereum’s urgent response, and growing investor anxiety—signals that quantum is being taken seriously as infrastructure source . The technical advances in error correction (Alice & Bob’s 10,000x improvement, EdenCode’s real-time AI decoding) and hardware scaling (Columbia’s 360,000 optical tweezers, Russia’s 72-qubit prototype) show the field is solving real engineering challenges source .

The funding momentum (Equal1’s $60M, Photonic’s $130M) reflects investor confidence in specific technical approaches, particularly silicon-based systems compatible with existing semiconductor manufacturing source . The open-source initiative (Open Quantum Design) and workforce development efforts (Colorado School of Mines degree) address the democratization and talent pipeline challenges source .

But the IBM study reveals a persistent enterprise readiness gap: 59% expect quantum AI to transform industries by 2030, but only 27% are preparing to use it source . Only 10% of companies have a roadmap to address quantum risks, despite 71% expecting attacks within five years source .

The pattern is clear: quantum computing is maturing from science project to infrastructure, but adoption will lag behind capability. 2026 is about preparing for a quantum future that’s still years away, but no longer theoretical. As Quandela’s trends indicate, the focus has shifted to hybrid quantum-classical computing, industrial use cases, error correction, and cybersecurity source . The question is no longer whether quantum computing will arrive, but whether organizations will be ready when it does.
