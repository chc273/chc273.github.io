May 2025 brought significant advances in quantum error correction, with multiple research groups demonstrating techniques that could dramatically reduce the resources needed for fault-tolerant quantum computing. At the same time, new systems came online, major funding rounds were announced, and the industry continued to mature with strategic partnerships and acquisitions. This comprehensive review covers 198 articles published in May 2025.

Error Correction Advances¶

Qudit Correction Breaks New Ground¶

Researchers at Yale University have demonstrated the first experimental quantum error correction for qudits —quantum systems with more than two levels. The team achieved error correction for both qutrits (3-level systems) and ququarts (4-level systems) using the Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) bosonic code, surpassing the break-even point for the first time in higher-dimensional quantum information processing. The experiment, published in Nature , achieved performance gains of 1.82 ± 0.03 for qutrits and 1.87 ± 0.03 for ququarts.

This approach leverages the larger Hilbert space of higher-dimensional quantum systems, potentially enabling more hardware-efficient error correction. The researchers used a reinforcement learning agent to optimize the system and employed the small-big-small (SBS) protocol with echoed conditional displacement (ECD) gates. Key specifications included a cavity energy relaxation lifetime of 631 microseconds and cavity Ramsey coherence time of 1,030 microseconds.

Bosonic Codes Show Promise¶

Nord Quantique announced results from its multimode encoding approach using a bosonic Tesseract code, demonstrating no measurable decay over 32 correction cycles . The technique uses a 1:1 ratio between physical cavities and logical qubits, dramatically reducing the physical qubit overhead typically required for error correction.

The company projects that systems with over 1,000 logical qubits could occupy just 20 square meters by 2029. For the RSA-830 benchmark, the approach demonstrated a 99.6% reduction in power consumption compared to classical HPC—120 kWh projected versus 280,000 kWh for conventional methods. The system achieved a 1 MHz clock speed, providing an efficiency edge over many competing approaches.

RSA Attack Requirements Drop¶

Craig Gidney, a researcher at Google Quantum AI, has published a revised estimate for the resources needed to crack RSA-2048 encryption. The new analysis indicates that fewer than 1 million physical qubits could break a 2048-bit RSA key in less than a week—a 95% reduction from the 20 million qubit estimate Gidney published in 2019.

The improvement stems from advances in approximate modular exponentiation, layered error correction, and optimized quantum operations. The study assumes a surface code cycle time of 1 microsecond, maximum gate error rate of 0.1%, and approximately 6.5 billion Toffoli gate operations requiring about 5 days of continuous operation. This puts increased pressure on organizations to accelerate migration to post-quantum cryptography, with NIST recommending deprecation of vulnerable systems after 2030 .

Additional Error Correction Advances¶

Infleqtion, in collaboration with JPMorgan Chase, has released an open-source quantum error correction library called qLDPC that implements low-density parity check codes. The library can reduce the number of physical qubits needed per logical qubit by a factor of 10 to 100—from approximately 1,500 physical qubits per logical qubit to just 15-150 physical qubits.

Researchers also published work on concatenated codes combining C4/C6 codes with quantum Hamming codes , which reduces space overhead by over 90% compared to surface codes for logical error rates of 10^-10 and 10^-24, while achieving a high threshold of 2.5%. This protocol is particularly suited for quantum architectures with all-to-all connectivity, such as neutral atoms and trapped ions.

New Systems and Milestones¶

Commercial Systems Launch¶

D-Wave announced general availability of the Advantage2 quantum computer , featuring 4,400+ qubits and operating at 12.5 kilowatts of power consumption. The sixth-generation system has processed 20.6 million customer problems on prototypes since June 2022, with usage increasing 134% in the past six months. The system is available through D-Wave’s Leap cloud service in 40 countries with 99.9% availability, and can also be deployed on-premises at customer facilities.

Equal1 launched the Bell-1 quantum server , the world’s first silicon-based quantum computer that is rack-mountable and plugs into a standard power outlet. The six-qubit system weighs 440 pounds (200 kg) and operates at 0.3 kelvin, using a self-contained cooling unit that eliminates the need for specialized infrastructure. The design integrates classical processors with quantum processor units on a single chip, allowing deployment in existing data centers.

Quandela launched the Belenos photonic quantum computer , featuring a doubling of qubit count and a 4,000× power increase compared to its predecessor.

In China, Origin Quantum released the Tianji 4.0 , a superconducting quantum measurement and control system that supports over 500 qubits. The Origin Wukong quantum computer, powered by Tianji technology, has served users in 139 countries and regions, completing 380,000 quantum computing tasks across industries like finance and biomedicine since beginning operation in January 2024.

National Deployments¶

IQM Quantum Computers signed an agreement with Finland’s VTT Technical Research Centre to deliver a 150-qubit system in mid-2026 and a 300-qubit system in late 2027 . The 300-qubit system will be the largest superconducting quantum computer procured to date and is purpose-built for quantum error correction research. The project builds on previous deliveries of 5-qubit, 20-qubit, and 50-qubit systems to VTT.

QuEra Computing installed its first quantum computer outside its own labs at Japan’s National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST). The gate-based neutral-atom system will be integrated with the NVIDIA-powered ABCI-Q supercomputer as part of Japan’s national quantum strategy. Japanese Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba declared 2025 “the first year of quantum industrialisation.”

In Italy, the first IQM quantum computer was activated in Turin at Politecnico di Torino. The five-qubit system operates at 20 millikelvin, occupies 4 square meters, and stands 3 meters tall. IQM has also signed an agreement with CINECA to integrate a 54-qubit Radiance quantum computer with the Leonardo supercomputer by Q4 2025.

India is deploying its largest quantum computer, an IBM Quantum System Two with a 156-qubit Heron processor , in Amaravati through collaboration between IBM, TCS, and the Andhra Pradesh government. The Quantum Valley Tech Park is scheduled to open in January 2026.

Performance Benchmarks¶

Quantinuum announced that its H2 system has achieved a Quantum Volume of 8,388,608 (2^23) , the highest reported QV to date. This milestone concludes the company’s five-year commitment to increase QV tenfold annually. The company’s next-generation Helios system is expected to surpass H2 in scale and fidelity.

Funding, Acquisitions, and Partnerships¶

Strategic Acquisitions¶

IonQ announced its intention to acquire Lightsynq , a startup specializing in photonic interconnects and quantum repeaters. Lightsynq brings a team of experts from Harvard and AWS, along with a portfolio of over 20 quantum memory patents. The acquisition positions IonQ to pursue modular scaling toward millions of qubits and to develop quantum internet infrastructure. IonQ’s current Forte systems feature 36 algorithmic qubits, with roadmap targets scaling to the 10,000s and ultimately to millions of qubits.

Major Funding Rounds¶

NVIDIA is in advanced talks to invest in photonic quantum computing startup PsiQuantum , marking the GPU giant’s first direct bet on hardware qubits. The potential investment would value PsiQuantum near $6 billion in a $750 million funding round led by BlackRock. PsiQuantum aims to build a million-qubit fault-tolerant quantum computer by 2028 using photonic integrated circuits fabricated in commercial CMOS lines. The company has previously secured $940 million from Australia and over $500 million from the State of Illinois for its quantum campus.

Classiq Technologies raised $110 million in a funding round led by Entrée Capital, bringing total capital raised to $173 million. The Israeli quantum software startup aims to become the “Microsoft of quantum computing” by providing a universal software platform for quantum applications. The funding will support global expansion and workforce growth from 65 to 130 employees. This represents part of a broader surge in Israeli quantum funding, with $289 million raised by Israeli quantum computing startups in 2025 compared to $82 million in 2024.

Maryland Governor Wes Moore has committed $1 billion over five years to establish the state as the “‘Capital of Quantum’” through a combination of state funding and federal matching. The initiative includes up to $100 million in matching contributions each from DARPA and Maryland through 2029, plus $10 million in state funding for IonQ’s new headquarters in College Park and an additional $5 million in economic development funds.

Other significant funding announcements include Alice & Bob building a $50 million quantum lab in Paris and Quobly raising €21 million for development of a 100-qubit silicon chip.

Strategic Partnerships¶

Invest Qatar entered a strategic partnership with Quantinuum to strengthen the country’s quantum ecosystem. The agreement includes tailored support services, joint research initiatives, and educational programs. This follows the launch of Qatar’s first quantum computing laboratory at Hamad Bin Khalifa University under the QC2 initiative, supported by a $10 million research grant from Qatar’s Ministry of Defence.

Kvantify, a Danish quantum software company, will use the AI supercomputer Gefion to simulate quantum computers at scale for quantum chemistry and drug discovery. This marks the first time a dedicated quantum software company leverages Gefion’s exaflop-class performance. The collaboration enables large-scale simulations of industry-relevant molecules like penicillin using hundreds of qubits.

Honda and Quemix co-developed a quantum state readout technology that “scans” classical data features from a quantum state without causing collapse. The partners performed the world’s first practical XAFS computation for materials development on an actual quantum computer using logical qubits, aiming to accelerate research into energy materials for carbon neutrality by Honda’s 2050 target.

In India, the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) inaugurated the Quantum Technology Research Centre in Delhi , focused on developing sovereign quantum technologies for defense applications including secure communications, sensing, and precision timing.

Miami University and Cleveland Clinic are partnering to establish Ohio’s first specialized quantum computing degree programs and research experiences, leveraging Cleveland Clinic’s IBM Quantum System One—the world’s first quantum computer dedicated to healthcare research.

Research Frontiers¶

Error Sources and Mitigation¶

A study published in Nature has directly observed that cosmic-ray muons and gamma-rays cause quasiparticle bursts in superconducting qubits, leading to large-scale correlated errors that are devastating for quantum error correction. Researchers separated the contributions of muons (18.4%) and gamma-rays (81.6%) and demonstrated that monitoring charge-parity jumps provides a highly sensitive method for detecting these bursts. The study, which used a 63-qubit superconducting processor with 31 qubits selected for measurement, estimates that muon-induced bit flips occur approximately every 25 minutes.

This research highlights a fundamental obstacle to practical fault-tolerant quantum computing with superconducting qubits. DARPA is currently investigating which quantum approaches can overcome these correlated error challenges.

Qubit Platform Advances¶

Silicon spin qubits continue to gain ground as a leading candidate for scalable quantum computing. A review in Intelligent Computing details advances in gate-defined and donor-based quantum dots, highlighting 0.5 second coherence times and 99.95% single-qubit gate fidelity. The technology’s compatibility with semiconductor manufacturing enables potential operation above 1 Kelvin (“hot qubits”), which would significantly reduce cooling requirements.

Researchers at the University of Oxford have developed a novel experimental technique, the Andreev STM, to definitively identify intrinsic topological superconductors . The technique confirmed uranium ditelluride (UTe2) as an intrinsic topological superconductor, accelerating the search for materials that can host stable, topologically protected qubits. This could enable the discovery of simple, inexpensive crystalline materials to replace complex synthetic structures.

Researchers from the University of Illinois Chicago demonstrated that combining Majorana edge modes and Majorana zero modes in two-dimensional topological superconductors provides a platform for efficient fault-tolerant quantum gates. The work shows how Majorana edge modes can function as memory elements, simulating key quantum operations through many-body dynamics.

MIT researchers developed a new superconducting circuit component called the “quarton coupler” that enables record-breaking interaction between light and matter, potentially making quantum operations and readout up to 10 times faster—allowing for more error correction within qubits’ limited coherence time.

Researchers at Florida State University discovered exotic states of matter in graphene , including integer and fractional quantum anomalous Hall states that allow electrons to flow without resistance at zero magnetic field. The discovery was made by engineering five-layer graphene structures with boron nitride at ultra-low temperatures.

Algorithms and Applications¶

A benchmarking study published in Nature demonstrated that quantum annealing with advanced hardware and hybrid techniques outperforms classical solvers for large-scale combinatorial optimization problems. The quantum solver achieved approximately 0.013% higher accuracy and was about 6,561 times faster than the best classical solver on a 10,000-variable problem, solving it in 0.0854 seconds.

Researchers used quantum computing and generative machine learning to design novel KRAS inhibitors for cancer treatment . The hybrid AI framework screened over 1 million molecules, identifying 15 candidates, two of which showed binding and functional activity in cellular assays. The work used Quantum Circuit Born Machines (QCBMs) combined with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks.

Australian researchers used a trapped-ion quantum computer and a novel mixed qudit-boson simulation technique to model how real molecules change after absorbing light. The approach is at least 1 million times more resource-efficient than standard quantum methods, successfully simulating light-induced dynamics in three molecules using far fewer qubits and entangling operations.

Researchers introduced AdaBoost.Q, a quantum adaptation of the AdaBoost algorithm , achieving over 86% accuracy on a ten-class MNIST task and 100% accuracy on a quantum feature recognition task using a programmable superconducting processor.

IBM released a quantum optimization benchmarking library (QOBLIB) with 10 problem instances to advance the field. Researchers also developed a greedy gradient-free adaptive VQE algorithm that demonstrates noise resistance for near-term quantum devices.

Other research advances include:

  • Quantum computing predicting particle trajectories in optical tweezers using linear combination of unitaries
  • Synthesis of a reversible quantum Vedic multiplier on IBM quantum computers with significant reductions in quantum cost
  • First end-to-end QEC pipeline for molecular energy on Quantinuum H2-2
  • Lockheed Martin and IBM combining quantum with classical HPC using SQD

Networking and Communication¶

Researchers have demonstrated universal distributed blind quantum computing with solid-state qubits , enabling secure quantum computations where the client’s data remains private from the server.

Scientists in the U.K. successfully connected two quantum processors using existing fiber optic cables for the first time. The experiment in distributed quantum computing allowed the processors to share resources and operate as a single entity, transmitting photons, quantum information, and a quantum algorithm (Grover search).

Researchers from the University of Geneva demonstrated that joint quantum measurements can be performed on separate particles without physical proximity, as long as measurement devices share entangled particles. They developed a classification system that maps measurement types to the required entanglement resources.

Materials and Novel Approaches¶

Researchers are exploring the fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect (FQAHE) in materials like twisted bilayer MoTe2 and rhombohedral multilayer graphene as a pathway to create topological qubits with intrinsic fault tolerance. The work outlines two potential routes to achieve Z3 parafermions, which are necessary for universal topological quantum computation.

Researchers have proposed a complete architecture for fault-tolerant continuous-variable quantum computation based on a 2D spatiotemporal cluster state. The architecture combines a biased Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) code with a repetition code, achieving fault tolerance with a squeezing threshold of 12.3 dB.

Norwegian researchers discovered that common clay exhibits a 2D structure suitable for quantum technology, specifically the mineral dickite, which could enable more affordable production of quantum components.

Ecosystem Expansion¶

New Research Centers¶

Hamad Bin Khalifa University has launched Qatar’s first quantum computing laboratory under the Qatar Center for Quantum Computing (QC2) initiative. The lab is supported by a $10 million research grant from Qatar’s Ministry of Defence and will focus on applied research in quantum algorithms, error correction, cryptography, and benchmarking.

India’s Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) inaugurated the Quantum Technology Research Centre in Delhi , focused on developing sovereign quantum technologies for defense applications like secure communications, sensing, and precision timing.

Global Quantum Developments¶

The United Nations has declared 2025 the International Year of Quantum Science and Technology, with 14 countries submitting the resolution and 70 countries backing it. In a historic milestone, researchers in South Africa achieved the first quantum satellite communication in the southern hemisphere, setting a record for the longest intercontinental link at 12,900 km. South Africa’s Department of Science and Technology has provided ZAR142 million (USD $7.8 million) in funding for the South African Quantum Technology Initiative over five years.

Africa’s quantum ambitions are expanding, with continental collaboration and national strategies emerging to develop a quantum economy. The African Quantum Consortium (AQC) hosted an online roundtable in March 2025, and the Quantum Science and Technology Across Africa conference is scheduled for June 2025 at the University of KwaZulu-Natal.

Policy and Industry Analysis¶

The U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency’s 2025 Worldwide Threat Assessment warns that quantum technologies , particularly sensors and secure communications, are nearing battlefield relevance as China and Russia make significant advances. The report highlights that quantum sensing could undermine traditional stealth and navigation capabilities.

Science News examined “the second quantum revolution” as quantum mechanics turns 100 years old, tracing its origins to Werner Heisenberg’s 1925 work and highlighting current efforts to precisely control quantum phenomena for building quantum computers, sensors, and communication networks.

Wipro released analysis on quantum computing becoming a scaled solution, while Live Science published a primer on quantum computing facts for general audiences. Silicon Quantum Computing CEO Michelle Simmons discussed the industry landscape on Bloomberg.

Looking Ahead¶

May’s developments underscore a key theme in quantum computing: multiple approaches to error correction are showing improved efficiency simultaneously. From Yale’s qudit demonstration and Nord Quantique’s bosonic codes to Infleqtion’s qLDPC library and Google’s revised RSA attack estimate, the field is finding ways to reduce the overhead required for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Hardware diversification continues with no clear winner yet. Silicon spin qubits gain ground due to semiconductor compatibility; photonic systems attract major NVIDIA investment; annealing systems from D-Wave reach general availability; neutral atoms from QuEra deploy internationally; and trapped-ion systems from Quantinuum set benchmark records. This experimentation suggests the market may support multiple specialized architectures rather than converging on a single technology.

Strategic consolidation through acquisitions like IonQ-Lightsynq and partnerships like Honda-Quemix shows investor confidence maturing beyond early hype. Government investments are moving from announcement to deployment: Finland’s 300-qubit system is contracted for 2027, India’s IBM system is deploying, and Maryland’s $1 billion initiative is executing.

The timeline to useful quantum computers remains long, but the path is becoming more concrete. Error correction requirements are decreasing; hardware options are expanding; and funding is flowing to both research and commercialization. The question is shifting from “when” to “what applications first” as focus narrows to optimization, chemistry simulations, and materials science as potential early advantage domains.

Challenges remain: cosmic rays cause correlated errors in superconducting arrays; scaling from hundreds to millions of qubits requires modular approaches; and post-quantum cryptography migration timelines are compressing. But May’s progress suggests the field is making tangible advances on the technical obstacles that have long separated quantum promise from practical reality.
