IBM unveiled a decoder that achieves “orders of magnitude” better error correction, Fujitsu launched a bold 10,000-qubit roadmap targeting 2030, and a corrected paper rekindled controversy over Microsoft’s $1 billion Majorana qubit program. Meanwhile, the industry accelerated toward practical quantum computing with new partnerships, product launches, and enterprise adoption.

This comprehensive review covers 165 articles published during August 2025, surveying the latest developments across error correction breakthroughs, scaling roadmaps, scientific debate, research advances, product launches, and industry maturation.

The Error Correction Breakthrough¶

The single most significant technical development of August came from IBM, which unveiled Relay-BP , a new decoder for quantum Low-Density Parity Check (qLDPC) codes that achieves logical error rates “orders of magnitude” better than previous state-of-the-art decoders. The algorithm delivers a 10x improvement in accuracy compared to the BP+OSD decoder while being flexible and compact enough for real-time FPGA implementation .

Relay-BP addresses the core challenge that makes fault-tolerant quantum computing so difficult: qubits are fragile, and error correction requires decoding in real time as the quantum computer runs. Previous decoders either lacked accuracy or were too computationally expensive to implement in hardware. IBM’s solution uses a message-passing algorithm that can be implemented on FPGAs, bringing practical decoding closer to reality.

The breakthrough could accelerate the timeline for practical quantum computing by a decade by reducing the resources required for error correction by 90% . IBM researchers published a preprint detailing Relay-BP on arXiv in July , and testing on hardware with the Kookaburra system could begin as early as 2026.

August saw multiple complementary advances in error correction. Alice & Bob, working with researchers at Inria, demonstrated an “unfolded distillation” protocol that dramatically reduces the resources needed to prepare magic states—essential components for fault-tolerant universal quantum computation. Their new “Heart Code” requires 8.7x fewer qubits than leading approaches and achieves a logical error rate of 10⁻⁷ in simulation. The method completes in 5.5 rounds of error correction —using an order of magnitude fewer qubit cycles than state-of-the-art schemes.

A study published in Science Advances on August 13 proposed a measurement-free approach to scalable, fault-tolerant universal quantum computing. By eliminating the need for measurements during computation, this method could reduce the complexity and overhead of quantum error correction.

These advances collectively address the biggest bottleneck in quantum computing: error correction remains the primary obstacle between today’s noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices and the fault-tolerant systems needed for practical advantage. While significant engineering challenges remain, August provided multiple credible paths forward.

The Race to Scale: Fujitsu’s 10,000-Qubit Target¶

On August 1, Fujitsu announced a five-year initiative to develop a superconducting quantum computer with 10,000+ physical qubits by fiscal 2030 , targeting 250 logical qubits using the company’s STAR architecture. This represents one of the most ambitious scaling roadmaps in the industry, backed by Japan’s NEDO and involving collaboration with Osaka University, AIST, and RIKEN.

The STAR (Space-Time efficient Analog Rotation) architecture, unveiled in August 2024 , is designed as an early fault-tolerant system. Fujitsu’s roadmap extends beyond the 2030 target: the company aims for 1,000 logical qubits by fiscal 2035 and has an ultimate goal of a fully fault-tolerant system with 1 million qubits . The company notes that 60,000 qubits using STAR architecture would be sufficient to outperform conventional computers.

Fujitsu has a track record of delivering on its qubit targets: a 64-qubit system was unveiled in October 2023 , and a 256-qubit “world-leading” system was introduced in April 2025 . The new roadmap represents a dramatic acceleration from those milestones.

Beyond Fujitsu’s announcement, modular architectures and chip-linking technologies emerged as a key scaling strategy across multiple research groups. Researchers at the University of California, Riverside simulated scalable quantum architectures and found that imperfect links between quantum chips can still produce fault-tolerant systems . Their work shows that links up to 10 times noisier than the chips themselves allow error correction to work, enabling larger quantum computers without requiring perfect hardware.

Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign demonstrated a modular architecture for superconducting quantum processors, achieving approximately 99% SWAP gate fidelity . The method uses detachable modules connected by superconducting coaxial cables, allowing for reconfiguration and scalability—published in Nature Electronics .

A simulation framework from Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya and Tsinghua University extended the NetSquid simulator with realistic noise models for cavity-based networks, revealing critical trade-offs between speed, accuracy, and reliability in quantum state transfer between chips.

Scaling is no longer just about qubit counts—architectures and interconnects matter equally. The modularity approach allows quantum computers to scale beyond what’s possible on a single chip while maintaining error correction capabilities.

Scientific Debate: Microsoft’s Majorana Controversy Deepens¶

On August 14, Science published a correction to a controversial 2020 paper co-authored by Microsoft-sponsored researchers who claimed to have conjured up Majorana particles in nanowires—a key step toward topological qubits. The correction, which follows recommendations from a University of Copenhagen investigation that found no scientific misconduct , has rekindled debate rather than resolving it.

Critics including Sergey Frolov of the University of Pittsburgh and Vincent Mourik of the Jülich Research Center continue to call for retraction, arguing that the paper cherry-picked data. The correction clarifies several terms and protocols and expands a catalog of experimental data, but the fundamental dispute remains: do the devices actually exhibit Majorana particles, or are researchers observing noise that mimics the expected signature?

The stakes are high. Microsoft has invested more than $1 billion in Majorana research, betting that topological qubits will be more stable than other approaches. In February 2025, the company announced the Majorana 1 chip with 8 Majorana particles acting as two qubits . A July 2025 preprint described experiments claiming one qubit functioned correctly for a few fractions of a second .

Independent experts remain skeptical. Henry Legg of the University of St. Andrews noted that “there’s no evidence for even the basic physics existing in these devices.” Jay Sau of the University of Maryland observed that “there is definitely a toxic cloud around the word ‘Majorana’ because of all this.”

Microsoft researchers, including Technical Fellow Chetan Nayak, continue their work, stating they understand their devices well and can distinguish true Majorana signals from “fake signatures” that mimic them.

This controversy illustrates the scientific process playing out in corporate quantum research. Validating exotic quantum states is extraordinarily difficult, and multiple high-profile Majorana claims have been retracted over the years. The intensified scrutiny reflects both the technical challenge and the massive investment riding on the approach.

Research Roundup: Materials, Architectures, and Novel Approaches¶

Beyond the major headlines, August saw remarkable breadth in quantum computing research across materials, architectures, algorithms, and applications.

Materials & Novel Qubit Approaches¶

Researchers from Chalmers University of Technology, Aalto University, and the University of Helsinki developed a new method using magnetism to create stable topological excitations in qubits. The approach leverages magnetic interactions rather than the rarer spin-orbit coupling, which could make quantum computers significantly more resilient to environmental disturbances.

Scientists from USC and Medgar Evers College discovered a previously overlooked particle called “neglectons” that, when combined with Ising anyons , enables universal quantum computation through braiding alone. Published in Nature Communications, this work emerges from a new mathematical framework called non-semisimple topological quantum field theories, which retains components traditionally discarded in the field.

Researchers at Penn State and Colorado State demonstrated that gold nanoclusters, called “super atoms” , can achieve up to 40% spin polarization and can be synthesized in large quantities. This offers a scalable platform for quantum applications as an alternative to fragile trapped-ion systems.

Scientists in Japan discovered that “heavy electrons” in a crystalline material called CeRhSn enter a “non-Fermi liquid” state and obey a universal speed limit known as Planckian scaling. The phenomenon occurs at temperatures below 80 Kelvin and suggests heavy-electron compounds could provide a new platform for quantum computers that harness collective electron motion.

Researchers from the University of Chicago demonstrated that enhanced yellow fluorescent protein (EYFP) can function as an optically addressable spin qubit , working even inside living cells. The study, published in Nature, opens a path to genetically encodable quantum sensors for biological systems.

Peking University researchers discovered a new form of symmetry-protected non-Abelian (SPNA) statistics within one-dimensional, strongly correlated bosonic systems, which could enable more robust encoding and manipulation of quantum information. The team also proposed a practical tri-junction implementation .

Researchers published in Nature proposed a new framework for topological quantum computation using non-semisimple topological quantum field theories, which introduces a new anyon type that allows universal quantum computation through braiding alone with Ising anyons —overcoming previous limitations that required additional operations beyond braiding.

Architecture & Hardware Advances¶

University of Basel researchers developed a method to make spin qubits faster and more robust by using spin-orbit coupling in a nanowire. They achieved a fourfold increase in coherence time and a threefold increase in drive speed while operating at a relatively warm 1.5 kelvin —significantly warmer than the typical less than 100 millikelvin required by most qubits.

Caltech researchers developed a hybrid quantum memory system that uses sound waves to store quantum information from superconducting qubits. The method allows quantum states to be stored up to 30 times longer than previous techniques by converting electrical signals into acoustic waves via a mechanical oscillator.

Researchers from Columbia Engineering developed HyperQ, a virtualization system that allows a single quantum computer to run multiple programs simultaneously by dividing its hardware into isolated quantum virtual machines. Tested on IBM’s 127-qubit Brisbane computer , the system reduced average user wait times by up to 40 times and increased program execution tenfold .

Engineers at the University of Pennsylvania successfully transmitted quantum signals over a live commercial fiber-optic internet network (Verizon’s) without losing their quantum state. They achieved this using a “Q-Chip” that bundles quantum and classical internet signals together, using the standard signal for routing and error correction.

Researchers at the University of Innsbruck created a quantum network node where individual qubits stored in trapped calcium ions are entangled with separate photons . They demonstrated this for a register of up to 10 qubits with 92% entanglement fidelity , showing a scalable approach for linking quantum computers and sensors.

Researchers demonstrated temporal fusion of entangled resource states generated by a solid-state quantum dot spin-photon interface. By fusing consecutive spin-photon Bell states generated from the same quantum emitter at different times separated by 300 ns , they create entanglement between the spin state at two different instances—drastically reducing the number of required physical quantum emitters.

A Chinese research team demonstrated a robust quantum advantage using the Jiuzhang 4.0 photonic quantum processor, which performed Gaussian boson sampling with 1024 squeezed states in 25.6 microseconds —a task estimated to take a classical supercomputer over 10⁴² years . The system achieved 92% efficiency in squeezed state generation.

Chinese researchers led by Pan Jianwei created the world’s largest atom array with over 2,000 rubidium atoms for quantum computing. They used AI and optical tweezers to achieve high accuracy in qubit operations, completing the array in 60 milliseconds .

Applications, Algorithms & Security¶

IBM and Moderna researchers used a quantum simulation algorithm to predict the secondary protein structure of a 60-nucleotide-long mRNA sequence —the longest such simulation ever performed on a quantum computer. The team demonstrated using up to 156 qubits and suggested scaling to 354 qubits in ideal conditions could improve accuracy.

Researchers published a hybrid quantum-classical method for solving robot inverse kinematics , using qubits to represent points on a sphere for forward kinematics. They demonstrated that a 2-qubit rotation gate can represent end-effector position, accelerating convergence and validating the approach on an actual quantum computer.

Researchers demonstrated quantum annealing for feature selection on medical image datasets , using D-Wave’s quantum annealer with 5,627 qubits and 40,279 couplings in the Pegasus topology. The method showed effectiveness in image reconstruction problems, offering better interpretability than learned representations like autoencoders.

RIKEN researchers used two 20-qubit quantum computers to simulate quantum information scrambling—a process key to understanding quantum systems like black holes. Published in Physical Review Research, the study illustrates a potential application for future quantum computers in performing simulations that may become too complex for classical computers.

Researchers at Northwestern University demonstrated a new security attack called QubitVise that exploits crosstalk in superconducting quantum computers to deliberately corrupt the computations of other users on shared cloud platforms. The double-sided attack, validated on Rigetti’s Ankaa-3 processor via Amazon Braket, successfully altered victim circuit outputs with an average increase of 13% in total variation distance —raising alarms about multi-tenant quantum infrastructure security.

Researchers introduced a “Quantum Tree Generator” (QTG) algorithm to solve 0-1 knapsack problems by generating all feasible solutions in superposition and using amplitude amplification to find optimal solutions. The work projects potential quantum advantage for instances with 100-600 variables under assumptions of fully connected logical qubits.

Researchers developed two quantum granular-ball generation methods that leverage quantum computing to accelerate the process, applied to a quantum k-nearest neighbors algorithm (QGBkNN). One method uses iterative splitting and the other a fixed number of splits, reducing time complexity for machine learning applications.

Product & Platform News: New Systems, Tools, and Cloud Access¶

New Quantum Computers¶

Rigetti Computing made its 36-qubit Cepheus-1-36Q quantum computer available in the cloud . The system uses a multi-chip design with four chiplets , reducing two-qubit gate error rates by 2x compared to Ankaa-3 and achieving 99% to 99.5% median two-qubit gate fidelity . The company plans to release a 100+ qubit system by the end of 2025 .

Japan launched a fully domestically produced superconducting quantum computer at The University of Osaka’s Center for Quantum Information and Quantum Biology. The system, built entirely with domestic components , marks a significant step in Japan’s technological self-reliance. It went live on July 28, 2025 and was showcased at Expo 2025 in Osaka , where the public could interact with it via cloud access. The system uses the open-source OQTOPUS software toolchain and operates at 10 millikelvin in a dilution refrigerator.

Software Tools & Platforms¶

D-Wave Quantum Inc. released an open-source toolkit to integrate quantum computing with AI training using the PyTorch framework . The toolkit focuses on training restricted Boltzmann machines for generative AI tasks, which can reduce time and energy consumption compared to classical methods. The release includes a demo and is part of D-Wave’s efforts to foster quantum-AI collaboration.

Amazon Braket launched “program sets”, a new feature that allows customers to bundle up to 100 quantum circuits into a single quantum task . This reduces processing overhead and execution time with speedups of up to 24x . The feature is particularly useful for algorithms like variational quantum eigensolvers and quantum machine learning models, and is currently available on superconducting QPUs from Rigetti and IQM.

Kothar launched FORGE, a browser-based quantum simulation platform that provides intuitive visualization and real-time feedback to help users build quantum circuits and design experiments without downloading software.

IBM updated its Quantum Credits program on a new platform, though details were limited in available sources.

NVIDIA released CUDA-QX 0.4, which focuses on error correction and algorithm development , providing tools for developers working on quantum computing applications.

Applications & Integration¶

MOTH launched Space Moths, a quantum computing-powered massively multiplayer online game where levels are generated on-demand by a quantum computer. Developed in collaboration with Onward Studios, the game uses quantum hardware from IBM Quantum, IQM Quantum Computers, and VTT . It was unveiled at Gamescom 2025, demonstrating quantum computing’s application in gaming and entertainment.

Mitsui & Co. launched QIDO, a quantum-integrated chemistry platform , in collaboration with Quantinuum and QSimulate. The platform blends quantum and classical computing to accelerate chemical research, with Mitsui’s established chemical industry network helping identify practical use cases.

Industry Moves: Partnerships, Funding, and Market Dynamics¶

Strategic Partnerships¶

IBM and AMD partnered to combine quantum computing with high-performance computing (HPC) to create quantum-centric supercomputing platforms. The collaboration will focus on developing hybrid quantum-classical workflows, with an initial demonstration planned for later this year . The partnership leverages IBM’s quantum systems and software with AMD’s CPUs, GPUs, and AI accelerators.

QuEra Computing and Deloitte formed an alliance to accelerate enterprise adoption of neutral-atom quantum computing . The partnership combines QuEra’s hardware and software platforms with Deloitte’s industry and implementation expertise. An early collaboration in life sciences demonstrated that quantum reservoir computing on QuEra’s hardware can outperform classical models for molecular property prediction.

Oak Ridge National Laboratory selected IQM Radiance, a 20-qubit superconducting quantum computer , as its first on-premises quantum computer to be integrated into its HPC systems. The system will be delivered by Q3 2025 and is upgradeable to higher qubit counts.

The Technology Innovation Institute (TII) entered a strategic partnership with Quantinuum, granting TII researchers access to Quantinuum’s systems, including the upcoming Helios generation. The collaboration aims to develop and benchmark quantum algorithms for applications in chemistry, materials science, and optimization.

Rigetti Computing deployed a 9-qubit Novera QPU at Montana State University’s QCORE facility, making MSU the first academic institution with an on-premises Rigetti quantum computer. The 12,600-square-foot facility is supported by approximately 60 organizations, including $44.7 million from the U.S. Air Force Research Laboratory .

Cleveland Clinic launched a new round of its Quantum Innovation Catalyzer Program, a competitive 12-month initiative for start-ups to explore quantum computing applications in healthcare and life sciences. Up to four selected companies will receive investment up to $250,000 and gain dedicated access to the IBM Quantum System One.

More than 30 researchers from leading tech companies and academic institutions collaborated on a landmark review paper on tensor networks for quantum computing, emphasizing them as a crucial bridge for hybrid quantum-classical approaches with 20x speed advantage in practical quantum chemistry applications.

Market Analysis & Timeline¶

IBM leads with 47% of disclosed quantum deal value between 2020-2025, according to market analysis covered in August. The data reflects IBM’s established position in enterprise quantum computing through its Quantum Network.

The quantum computing industry is transitioning to industrial applications in 2025 , with companies moving from pure research to identifying practical use cases. This shift is evidenced by growing partnerships between quantum hardware companies and industry consultants like Deloitte.

IBM and Google are targeting operational quantum computers by 2029, representing an aggressive timeline for fault-tolerant systems. Fujitsu’s 2030 target for 250 logical qubits aligns with this timeframe.

The Microsoft-Atom Computing QuNorth project in Denmark aims to build “Magne,” a quantum computer with 50 logical qubits supported by over 1,200 physical qubits . Construction begins in late 2025 with an operational target of early 2027, backed by $46 million each from EIFO and Novo Nordisk Foundation .

Funding & M&A¶

Qunova Computing secured $10 million in Series A funding to advance its HI-VQE quantum algorithm for chemical simulations. The round brings total funding to $15.8 million.

Photonic Inc. received a $1 million CAD grant from Canada’s Department of National Defence for quantum networking research, specifically for developing deployable quantum network prototypes for Canadian defence applications.

Roadrunner Venture Studios launched a $25 million initiative in New Mexico focused on accelerating quantum companies in the state, building on successful investments in quantum computing startups.

The Australian Defence Department awarded $9 million AUD in contracts for quantum and information warfare technologies, including projects on quantum sensing, navigation, and secure communications.

Strangeworks acquired Quantagonia, a German quantum computing software company, expanding Strangeworks’ presence in Europe and adding its optimization and quantum-inspired computing technologies to the platform.

Amazon took a stake in IonQ, though financial details were not disclosed in available sources, signaling continued cloud provider investment in quantum hardware.

Kansas University set up a quantum research lab in partnership with Brilliancy Quantum Brands, though specific investment amounts were not detailed in available reports.

Quantum Readiness¶

Microsoft’s Quantum Safe Program aims to make the company quantum-safe by 2033, reflecting growing awareness of post-quantum cryptography requirements as quantum computers advance.

Both IBM and Google have stated goals for operational quantum computers by 2029, creating competitive pressure and clear timelines for the industry.

Looking Ahead: What August 2025 Means for Quantum Computing¶

The error correction breakthroughs from IBM and Alice & Bob address the single biggest technical challenge holding back practical quantum computing. IBM’s Relay-BP decoder achieves the kind of improvement—orders of magnitude better logical error rates—that could reduce error correction overhead by 90% and accelerate practical quantum computing timelines by years.

The scaling roadmaps show industry confidence. Fujitsu’s 10,000-qubit target by 2030, Microsoft-Atom Computing’s Magne with 50 logical qubits by 2027, and the IBM-Google target of operational quantum computers by 2029 all represent ambitious but specific commitments. These aren’t vague aspirations—they’re funded programs with technical milestones.

The scientific debate around Microsoft’s Majorana research illustrates the rigor required. Validating exotic quantum states is extraordinarily difficult, and the intensified scrutiny reflects both the technical challenge and the massive investment riding on the approach. The fact that critics continue to call for retraction while Microsoft proceeds with research shows this is genuine scientific inquiry, not settled fact.

The breadth of research in August demonstrates innovation across multiple approaches: superconducting (IBM, Fujitsu, Rigetti), neutral-atom (QuEra), trapped-ion (Quantinuum), photonic (Jiuzhang 4.0), topological (Microsoft’s Majorana effort), and even biological (proteins as qubits). This diversity is healthy—different approaches may win for different applications.

The partnerships and product launches show the ecosystem maturing. IBM-AMD merging quantum with HPC/AI, QuEra-Deloitte bringing neutral-atom quantum to enterprise, IQM at ORNL integrating with HPC systems—these aren’t just research collaborations, they’re deployment partnerships. The gaming application (Space Moths) shows quantum computers delivering value today in consumer applications.

What does this mean for the quantum timeline? If error correction progress continues at the pace demonstrated in August, and if scaling roadmaps stay on track, fault-tolerant quantum computers capable of solving practical problems could emerge in the late 2020s or early 2030s. The technical challenges remain significant—qubit stability, error correction at scale, and system architecture all need continued engineering progress.

But August provided concrete progress on the hardest problems. The transition from research to industrial applications is underway, with measurable advances rather than marketing hype. Quantum computing in late 2025 is a field advancing through sustained engineering and scientific progress. The path forward is clearer, even if the distance remaining is substantial.
