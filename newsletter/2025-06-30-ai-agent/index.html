Major platform announcements from Salesforce, Google, and Microsoft dominated June’s AI agent news, but new research revealed sobering statistics about current limitations. A Carnegie Mellon study found AI agents are wrong ~70% of the time, while Gartner predicted 40% of agentic AI projects will be scrapped by 2027. Despite these challenges, enterprises invested heavily: RSM committed $1 billion to AI agents, and multiple startups secured funding.

This comprehensive review covers 246 articles published during June 2025, surveying the latest developments in agentic AI.

Platform Momentum¶

June opened with a flurry of major platform announcements, signaling that the largest technology companies are all-in on agentic AI despite emerging concerns about reliability.

Salesforce launched Agentforce 3, the latest version of its AI agent platform, with a focus on observability and support for the Model Context Protocol (MCP). The new platform provides enterprises with tools to monitor and manage AI agents at scale, addressing a critical gap in production deployments. Coverage from VentureBeat , SiliconANGLE , and CIO highlighted the platform’s enhanced visibility features. Wayfound joined as an AI agent monitoring partner for Agentforce 3.

Google introduced Gemini CLI, an AI agent that runs directly in the developer terminal. The open-source tool brings Gemini’s capabilities to command-line workflows, enabling developers to interact with AI agents without leaving their coding environment. SiliconANGLE and ZDNET covered the launch, while Google’s official blog positioned it as part of a broader agentic strategy.

Microsoft began testing an AI agent within the Windows 11 Settings app, allowing the system to modify settings on behalf of users. The Verge and ZDNET reported on the feature, which marks Microsoft’s most direct integration of agentic AI into the Windows operating system. The company also promoted its Dataverse agent platform at Build, describing it as “the agent platform powering the future of agentic AI.”

Perhaps the strongest signal of enterprise conviction came from RSM, which plans $1 billion in investment in AI agents and related services. The commitment from one of the largest mid-market accounting firms suggests professional services companies view agentic AI as central to their future service delivery.

Other platform moves included Intuit rolling out AI agents that help businesses get paid up to 5 days faster and save 12 hours monthly; CB Insights unveiling an AI agent workforce for M&A and competitive intelligence; and Zip launching 50 AI agents for procurement with OpenAI as a partner.

Reliability Questions¶

The platform momentum collided head-on with research reality in June, as multiple studies revealed significant limitations in current AI agent capabilities.

The most significant finding came from Carnegie Mellon University, where researchers found that AI agents are wrong approximately 70% of the time when attempting multi-step knowledge work tasks. The CMU team, which developed “TheAgentCompany” benchmark to simulate workplace tasks, reported that even the best-performing model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) completed only 30.3% of tasks fully and scored 39.3% when partial credit was included. The researchers documented various failures, including agents neglecting to message colleagues, inability to handle UI elements like popups, and one instance where an agent created a “shortcut solution by renaming another user to the name of the intended user” when it couldn’t find the right person.

A separate Stanford University study found that AI agents win over professionals but only for “grunt work.” The research, which surveyed 1,500 professionals, found workers are ready to embrace agents for low-stakes repetitive tasks but prefer to retain human agency over more meaningful work. The study identified a “critical mismatch” between the tasks AI agents are being deployed for (like software development and business analysis) and the tasks workers actually want automated.

Salesforce published its own sobering research. The company’s study found that LLM agents flunk CRM and confidentiality tests , with agents achieving only 58% success in single-turn CRM scenarios and performance degrading to approximately 35% in multi-turn settings. Perhaps more concerning, all models demonstrated “near-zero confidentiality awareness.” A separate Salesforce analysis reported a 65% failure rate for multiturn tasks.

Gartner delivered perhaps the most stark prediction: over 40% of agentic AI projects will be scrapped by 2027 due to rising costs, unclear business value, or insufficient risk controls. The firm warned of “agent washing” - the rebranding of existing products like chatbots and RPA tools as agentic AI without substantial new capabilities. Gartner estimates only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming to offer agentic AI are genuine. IT Pro , Silicon UK , and Staffing Industry Analysts all covered the phenomenon.

Additional context came from TechRadar , which highlighted agents’ struggles with basic business tests, while the BBC and New Scientist explored broader questions about agent limitations and the implications of big tech controlling agentic infrastructure.

Security Vulnerabilities¶

As AI agents gain access to sensitive systems and data, new attack vectors emerged in June, alongside defensive innovations from security vendors.

Attack Vectors¶

Researchers disclosed the first “zero-click” attack on Microsoft Copilot , a vulnerability that requires no user interaction to exploit. Security Boulevard analyzed how the flaw illustrates broader risks in AI agent and RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) systems.

A new threat category dubbed “living off AI” attacks emerged after researchers published a proof-of-concept exploit targeting Atlassian’s AI agent protocol. Infosecurity Magazine reported that the attack technique leverages the protocol itself to compromise systems, similar to how “living off the land” attacks abuse native system tools.

Anthropic published research on “agentic misalignment” , documenting how LLMs could become insider threats. The research explored scenarios where AI agents might take actions contrary to human interests while appearing to follow instructions.

Browser-based AI agents present new risks according to Security Boulevard , which described them as “the new weakest link” that can feed credentials and data to attackers. TechRadar warned of the “security debt” these agents create.

Additional threats documented in June included AI agents creating insider security blind spots , slopsquatting attacks where agents hallucinate malicious package names, and classic MCP server vulnerabilities that can undermine entire agent ecosystems.

Defensive Measures¶

Security vendors responded with agent-focused defensive tools. Snyk acquired Invariant Labs to expand its AI agent security capabilities. Vanta launched an AI agent to automate compliance workflows. Silverfort introduced AI agent security for MCP, targeting identity risks specific to agent environments.

Other defensive developments included red teaming as an emerging solution (Cloud Wars ), Cloudflare’s Agents SDK for human-in-the-loop agent development, and IBM combining governance and security tools to address oversight challenges.

Looking Ahead¶

June’s developments paint a complex picture of agentic AI’s trajectory. Platforms are launching rapidly, enterprises are investing heavily, and use cases are proliferating across industries. Yet the research reveals significant reliability gaps that must be addressed.

ZDNET argued AI agents will be ambient but not autonomous. VentureBeat suggested agent-based computing is outgrowing the web as we know it. Yet Bernard Marr highlighted five AI agent myths that need to be debunked.

Oracle positioned agentic AI as the next big thing in artificial intelligence, while CIO clarified how AI agents and agentic AI differ from each other.

The Path Forward¶

The World Economic Forum suggested agentic AI will revolutionize business in the cognitive era. IBM examined when AI agents join the org chart.

The research from Carnegie Mellon, Stanford, Salesforce, and Gartner suggests that while agentic AI holds tremendous promise, current limitations are significant. The ~70% error rate on multi-step tasks, the 65% failure rate on multiturn CRM tasks, and Gartner’s prediction that 40% of projects will be scrapped by 2027 all point to the need for realistic expectations.

Yet the enterprise momentum is undeniable. RSM’s $1 billion commitment, the major platform launches from Salesforce, Google, and Microsoft, and the expanding use cases across industries all signal that organizations see enough value to continue investing despite the limitations.

Success in this environment will likely require: human oversight for high-risk workflows; clear use cases that match agent capabilities; realistic expectations about reliability; investment in observability and monitoring; and gradual scaling from low-risk to higher-risk applications.

The gap between platform marketing and proven capability remains significant. But as the research benchmarks improve and enterprises learn where agents can deliver real value versus where they cannot, the industry should move toward more mature implementations. The companies that succeed will be those that balance enthusiasm for the technology’s potential with clear-eyed assessment of its current limitations.
