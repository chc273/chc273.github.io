This comprehensive review covers 285 articles published between March 1, 2025 and April 1, 2025, surveying a pivotal month for AI agents. OpenAI plans to charge $20,000 monthly for “PhD-level” AI agents while China’s Manus demonstrates fully autonomous capabilities. Every major tech company launched agent platforms, enterprises deployed agents at scale, and startups raised $93.8 million to solve critical infrastructure gaps.

The $20,000 Question¶

OpenAI is reportedly planning to charge up to $20,000 per month for specialized AI agents designed to perform “PhD-level” research work, according to a report in The Information . The reported pricing strategy includes three tiers: $2,000 monthly for “high-income knowledge workers,” $10,000 for software development agents, and the $20,000 tier for research-intensive tasks requiring doctoral-level expertise.

The pricing is based on performance metrics from OpenAI’s o3 and o3-mini models, which use what the company calls “private chain of thought” reasoning. According to Ars Technica , o3 scored 87.5% on the ARC-AGI visual reasoning benchmark (comparable to the 85% human threshold), 96.7% on the 2024 American Invitational Mathematics Exam (missing just one question), and 87.7% on GPQA Diamond, which contains graduate-level biology, physics, and chemistry questions. On the Frontier Math benchmark by EpochAI, o3 solved 25.2% of problems while no other model has exceeded 2%.

The reported pricing represents a dramatic departure from current consumer AI costs. ChatGPT Plus remains $20 per month, Claude Pro costs $30 monthly, and even ChatGPT Pro’s $200/month subscription is tiny compared to the proposed enterprise fees. As xAI developer Hieu Pham noted in a widely-shared tweet: “most PhD students, including the brightest stars who can do way better work than any current LLMs—are not paid $20K / month.”

Behind the pricing strategy is significant financial pressure. OpenAI reportedly lost approximately $5 billion last year covering operational costs. At the same time, investor commitment remains strong: SoftBank has reportedly committed $3 billion to spend on OpenAI’s agent products this year alone.

Alongside the pricing news, OpenAI unveiled new tools for building AI agents, including a “reasoning” API that enables developers to build agents that can “think before they speak” using the company’s o3 models. The eWeek reported that these tools represent a “powerful suite” to simplify AI agent development.

However, skepticism about the “PhD-level” framing has emerged. As the Indian Express noted , “The hype around PhD-level AI comes with growing scepticism.” The models still struggle with confabulations—instances where they generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect information—which remains a critical concern for research applications where accuracy matters.

The Autonomous Agent Arrival¶

While OpenAI focuses on premium pricing, a Chinese startup called Butterfly Effect has captured global attention with Manus, an AI agent described as the country’s first “fully autonomous” agent. According to Live Science , representatives claim Manus is “the world’s first general AI agent” designed to think, plan, and execute real-world tasks independently.

Unlike conventional AI tools that primarily assist users by providing recommendations or answering queries, Manus functions without human intervention . As The Economic Times explained , Manus “can come up with an itinerary for a trip to Japan, analyze stocks, and handle other complex tasks on its own.”

The emergence of Manus has drawn immediate comparisons to DeepSeek, another Chinese AI model that went viral earlier this year. As Axios reported , the new agent is “winning expert acclaim while stoking concern over another AI advance rooted in Chinese research and development.” Yicai Global claimed that Manus “is said to beat OpenAI’s Deep Research” in performance comparisons.

MIT Technology Review noted that researchers are actively testing Manus’s capabilities. The South China Morning Post reported that the startup’s founder believes Manus “transcends chatbots” in its ability to take independent action.

Manus added a mobile app and subscription plans in March, expanding access beyond web interfaces. Geeky Gadgets described how the agent is “redefining task automation and workflow management” through advanced automation, research, and coding capabilities.

The autonomous capabilities have also sparked discussion about how close we might be to artificial general intelligence (AGI). As Quartz noted , China now has a “completely autonomous” AI agent that operates differently from Western models. SiliconANGLE reported that the agent is “getting global attention for its autonomous smarts.”

Platform Wars: Big Tech’s Agent Offensive¶

March marked a coordinated offensive from every major technology company, each launching agent platforms aimed at enterprise dominance.

Microsoft: 400,000 Agents Deployed¶

Microsoft emerged as the early leader in the agent platform race. According to Cloud Wars , 160,000 customers have built 400,000 AI agents using the company’s Copilot platform. The scale demonstrates how quickly enterprises are moving beyond experimentation to deployment.

The company announced a range of specialized agents across different domains:

  • Security agents: Six new AI agents for security tasks, including phishing triage that can “handle routine phishing alerts and cyberattacks” and alert fatigue reduction that frees up human defenders
  • Factory automation: New agents for manufacturing environments announced at Hannover Messe 2025
  • Sales agents: AI Accelerator for Sales and new sales agents to accelerate AI-first selling
  • Research and data analysis: OpenAI-powered Copilot agents for workflow acceleration

Microsoft also expanded partnerships: Adobe joined to empower marketers with AI agents in Microsoft 365 Copilot, while OneTrust launched an AI agent built with Microsoft Security Copilot.

ZDNET reported on two new “deep reasoning” AI agents that can speed up workflows. The company is positioning its agents as alternatives to Salesforce CRM systems.

Amazon: Browser Control in Nova Act¶

Amazon entered the agent platform wars with Nova Act, an AI agent that can directly control web browsers to navigate websites and make purchases. According to The Verge , Amazon’s new agent “is designed to do your shopping.”

The browser-controlling capability represents a significant technical milestone. RetailWire reported that Nova Act “can take over a web browser and make purchases” on behalf of users. As PYMNTS noted , the agent can “shop and place orders” autonomously.

Beyond consumer applications, Amazon Web Services announced general availability of multi-agent collaboration on Bedrock, enabling multiple agents to work together on complex tasks. CNBC reported that the Nova launch “puts it up against rivals OpenAI, Anthropic.”

Salesforce: Agent Marketplace¶

Salesforce pursued an app store-style strategy with AgentExchange, a marketplace for AI agent skills launched with 200+ initial partners . As Techzine Global reported , the marketplace is “specifically focused on skill-sharing” between agents.

The company also launched Agentforce 2dx, which enables AI agents to run in the background rather than requiring explicit user prompts. According to Techzine Global , the update sets “a new standard” as “AI agents become proactive.”

InfoWorld noted that the update aims to simplify AI agent development and deployment. Salesforce DevOps reported that AgentExchange is designed to “drive adoption of role-based AI agents.”

In healthcare, Salesforce released pre-built AI agents to tackle “time-consuming healthcare tasks.”

Oracle: No-Code Agent Studio¶

Oracle introduced AI Agent Studio, a no-code tool for creating AI agents across its Fusion application suite. According to Oracle’s announcement , the studio is “available at no additional cost” and delivers “easy-to-use tools, including advanced testing, robust validation, and built-in security.”

ZDNET reported that the new tool is an “impressive next step for Oracle’s AI strategy.” Capacity Global noted that the studio enables businesses to “build custom AI agents.”

Oracle announced AI agents for financial crime detection, adding a “broad class of AI agents and agentic workflows” to its Investigation Hub Cloud Service. SiliconANGLE reported that customers can “create and modify AI agents across” the Fusion suite.

The company also announced general availability of its OCI Generative AI Agents Platform. As Techzine Global noted , Oracle is positioning Agent Studio for Fusion Cloud Applications.

ServiceNow: Pre-Built Agent Library¶

ServiceNow took a vertical-specific approach with its Yokohama platform release, which adds to “thousands of AI agents across CRM, HR, IT, and more,” according to the Business Wire announcement .

The company introduced AI agents built for the telecom industry . These agents, built with NVIDIA AI Enterprise software and DGX Cloud, are designed to “drive productivity across the entire service lifecycle.”

Diginomica reported that Yokohama is “unleashing pre-configured AI agents, everywhere.” CX Today noted that ServiceNow is “going big on industry-specific innovation” with new AI agents and the Quality 360 acquisition.

Constellation Research reported that the Yokohama release “ups AI agent game” with ServiceNow Studio, an AI-powered workspace for no-code, low-code, and pro-code agent development. Techzine Global noted that ServiceNow is introducing “new AI agents for telecoms.”

Other Platform Players¶

Several other companies announced agent platforms:

  • PwC launched an AI agent operating system to “revolutionize AI workflows for enterprises”
  • Workato launched its enterprise AI agent platform and acquired AI customer support vendor DeepConverse
  • EY announced the EY.ai Agentic Platform , created with NVIDIA AI, for tax, risk, and finance domains
  • NVIDIA launched a family of open reasoning AI models for building agentic AI platforms
  • Kore.ai announced an agent platform for “building, deploying, and orchestrating agentic applications”
  • WSO2 expanded AI agent capabilities with platform-wide AI-native updates
  • Accenture debuted an AI agent builder within its AI Refinery platform
  • Deloitte unveiled Zora AI , described as “agentic AI for tomorrow’s workforce”
  • Adobe launched the Adobe Experience Platform Agent Orchestrator for businesses to activate AI agents

The Browser Pattern: Agents That Navigate the Web¶

A clear pattern emerged in March: browser-controlling agents as a primary interface for AI automation.

Browser Use raised $17 million in a funding round to help AI agents navigate websites. As TechCrunch explained , the startup is “making it easier for AI ‘agents’ to navigate websites.” SiliconANGLE reported that the funding will help “steer AI agents through the internet.”

Opera introduced “Browser Operator,” a native AI agent integrated directly into the browser to perform tasks for users. The screenshot from the announcement shows the agent designed to handle web-based workflows without leaving the browser.

Amazon’s Nova Act represents the most ambitious browser-controlling agent to date, with the ability to navigate websites and make purchases directly. The capability matters because it allows agents to interact with existing web infrastructure without requiring API integrations.

However, browser control also creates security concerns. Arcade.dev raised $12 million in seed funding specifically “to solve the biggest security problem with AI Agents.” As the company noted, it is “the first company to help AI agents securely take real-world actions on behalf of a user.”

Security Double-Edged: Agents as Defender and Threat¶

Agents as Defenders¶

Microsoft led the charge on using AI agents for security, announcing six specialized security agents for Security Copilot . According to The Verge , the agents will “help overwhelmed humans” by handling routine security tasks.

Help Net Security reported that the new AI agents “take on phishing, patching, alert fatigue.” The phishing triage agent specifically can “handle routine phishing alerts and cyberattacks, freeing up human defenders.”

SailPoint unveiled Harbor Pilot, a set of AI-powered agents for identity security . The agents are designed to “help identity teams work smarter, respond faster and secure their organizations more efficiently.”

Alpha Vision announced it will showcase AI agent-powered security solutions at ISC West 2025, demonstrating how agents can enhance physical security.

Oracle brought AI agents to financial crime detection , adding agents and agentic workflows to its Investigation Hub Cloud Service.

Agents as Attackers¶

But security researchers also demonstrated how AI agents can be used maliciously. SC Media reported that OpenAI’s Operator AI agent “can be used in phishing attacks.” Researchers showed that the same browser-controlling capabilities that make agents useful for legitimate tasks also enable them to conduct autonomous phishing attacks .

The dual-use nature of agent capabilities creates a security dilemma. As Arcade.dev noted in its funding announcement, securely enabling AI agents to “take real-world actions on behalf of a user” is one of the most critical challenges facing the industry.

Vertical Agents: Healthcare, Finance, and Beyond¶

Healthcare¶

Salesforce released pre-built AI agents specifically designed to “tackle time-consuming healthcare tasks.” The agents target administrative workflows that consume provider time.

Wipro announced AI agent solutions for healthcare transformation , powered by Salesforce’s Agentforce platform.

Nature published a paper on “coordinated AI agents for advancing healthcare,” exploring how multi-agent systems can improve medical workflows.

Financial Services¶

Auquan launched an industry-first AI agent purpose-built for risk monitoring in financial services. As Corporate Compliance Insights noted , Auquan launched an “agentic AI risk agent for FinServ.”

Oscilar announced how it is transforming risk decisioning with AI agents, targeting financial risk assessment workflows.

Oracle expanded its financial crime detection agents , adding a broad class of AI agents to its Investigation Hub Cloud Service.

Other Industries¶

Telecommunications: ServiceNow introduced AI agents built for the telecom industry , designed to drive productivity across the entire service lifecycle.

Manufacturing: Microsoft launched new AI agents for factory automation at Hannover Messe 2025. Ford is using agents with NVIDIA for distributed inference with collaborative AI agents.

Real Estate: An AI agent claiming to be the “world’s first AI real estate agent” has generated $100M in sales . As The Express Tribune reported , the agent generated $100M in sales for Portugal real estate.

Retail: A Salesforce report found that 75% of retailers say AI agents will be “essential by 2026.” Amazon’s Nova Act directly targets retail shopping workflows.

Reality Check: Why Agents Struggle¶

Despite the platform launches and funding, critical voices reminded the industry that significant challenges remain.

A widely-cited article titled “Why AI Agents Suck So Bad” detailed frustrating experiences with support agents, highlighting “limitations of current RAG-based AI systems in handling complex customer” issues.

More than half of companies have already deployed autonomous AI agents, according to a study by PagerDuty covered by Quartz. But adoption doesn’t equal satisfaction—implementation challenges abound.

CIO reported that IT leaders are “bracing for the AI agent management challenge.” As organizations deploy more agents, managing them becomes increasingly complex.

Skepticism about “PhD-level” marketing claims continues. As the Indian Express noted , “The hype around PhD-level AI comes with growing scepticism.”

Hidden risks in agent adoption are emerging, including concerns about data silos impacting agent performance. Enterprises face real management complexity as agent deployments scale.

What This Month Means¶

March 2025 was when AI agents graduated from research project to product. The evidence is across several dimensions:

Pricing as value signal: OpenAI’s reported $20,000 monthly pricing suggests the company believes these systems can deliver substantial enterprise value—even if the “PhD-level” framing remains marketing rhetoric.

Autonomous capabilities are here: China’s Manus demonstrated what fully autonomous agents can do, planning complex tasks without human intervention and drawing comparisons to DeepSeek’s viral impact.

Platform ubiquity: Every major tech company—Microsoft, Amazon, Salesforce, Oracle, ServiceNow—now offers an agent platform. The land grab for enterprise mindshare is underway.

Infrastructure being built: Startup funding of $93.8 million in a single month shows where the ecosystem needs work: browser control, security, payments, compliance, and monitoring.

Enterprise adoption accelerating: Microsoft’s 400,000 agents built by 160,000 customers, the PagerDuty study finding more than half of companies using autonomous agents, and industry-specific deployments all point to real adoption.

But we’re still early. Reliability issues persist, management complexity is real, and the gap between marketing claims and actual capabilities—especially around “PhD-level” performance—remains significant.

The next 6-12 months will be about deployment at scale. Which platforms actually deliver value? Which use cases prove durable? The agent wars have begun, but the winner is far from determined.
