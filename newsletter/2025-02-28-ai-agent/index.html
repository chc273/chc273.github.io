This comprehensive review covers 118 articles published in February 2025, surveying the rapid emergence of AI agents as autonomous digital workers. OpenAI launched Operator, an AI agent that autonomously browses the web and completes tasks. Google unveiled an AI co-scientist for research. Meanwhile, Workday, ServiceNow, and SAP are racing to build infrastructure to manage fleets of AI agents as digital employees.

The Big Launches¶

OpenAI and Google led February’s product announcements, introducing AI agents that autonomously navigate the web and conduct research.

OpenAI launched Operator, an AI agent that browses the web and completes tasks on behalf of users. Unlike traditional chatbots, Operator autonomously navigates websites, fills out forms, and clicks buttons. The agent rolled out to ChatGPT Pro users in multiple countries in February, available for most Pro users at the $200/month tier. Operator uses a computer-vision model to “see” web pages and take action.

OpenAI also announced Deep Research, an AI agent powered by the o3 model that conducts multi-step research tasks. The agent achieves a 26.6% accuracy on “Humanity’s Last Exam” benchmark across 3,000 questions and 100 subjects. Deep Research browses the web for 5 to 30 minutes, synthesizing information from hundreds of sources into comprehensive reports with citations. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman described it as “like a superpower; experts on demand!” The capability launched first for Pro users with 100 queries per month , with expansion planned to Plus, Team, and Enterprise tiers.

Google introduced an AI co-scientist, a multi-agent system built with Gemini 2.0 designed to help scientists generate novel hypotheses and research proposals. The system uses specialized agents—Generation, Reflection, Ranking, Evolution, Proximity, and Meta-review—that mirror the scientific method. In biomedical applications, the AI co-scientist proposed three novel drug repurposing candidates for acute myeloid leukemia that were validated in laboratory experiments. The company is opening access through a Trusted Tester Program for research organizations.

Google also launched “Ask for Me,” an experimental AI agent that calls businesses on a user’s behalf. Currently available through Search Labs in the U.S., the agent calls mechanics and nail salons to inquire about pricing and availability. This represents a potential evolution of Google’s discontinued Duplex technology and signals a move toward machine-to-machine customer service interactions. Gartner predicts that 20% of contact center traffic could come from machine customers by 2026.

The browser-use agent space is seeing competition. Convergence’s Proxy agent scored 88% on the WebVoyager benchmark , compared to Operator’s 87%. Proxy offers an unlimited plan at $20/month versus Operator’s $200/month Pro tier, though OpenAI’s agent benefits from integration with the broader ChatGPT ecosystem.

Enterprise Platform Wars¶

The most significant trend in February was the race to build AI agent management infrastructure. Workday, ServiceNow, and SAP are positioning themselves as the systems of record for managing AI agents as digital employees.

Workday unveiled the Workday Agent System of Record, a platform to help organizations manage their entire fleet of AI agents. The system provides tools for governance, management, and optimization of digital workforces, including onboarding, role definition, cost tracking, and compliance. Workday processes over 800 billion transactions annually through its Illuminate AI platform, giving it deep visibility into workforce operations.

Workday also introduced role-based AI agents with configurable skills that can perform hundreds of individual tasks. New agents announced include contract, payroll, financial auditing, and policy agents. The approach treats AI agents like employees in an HR system—a shift from “using AI” to “managing a workforce of AI agents.” Workday serves as the system of record for 10,500 companies worldwide.

“The workforce of the future will include both humans and AI agents,” said Aneel Bhusri , Workday’s co-founder and executive chair. “Businesses that don’t learn to manage this incredibly complex reality will fall quickly behind.”

SAP unleashed several “ready-to-use” AI agents powered by its Joule virtual assistant. The agents are available across customer service, sales, and finance, including a Cash Collection agent that collaborates across departments to resolve disputes. SAP also introduced a custom agent builder in Joule Studio, allowing citizen developers to create tailored AI agents using a no-code tool. The company is positioning Joule as an “AI orchestrator” that can assemble teams of agents to perform complex end-to-end processes.

ServiceNow is positioning itself as the “control tower” for AI agents across IT, customer service, and HR workflows. Its AI Agent Orchestrator and AI Agent Studio enable organizations to build and manage agents with real-time access to enterprise data through the Workflow Data Fabric. The focus is on cross-functional enterprise automation, particularly in IT service management.

Oracle announced AI agents for HR through its Fusion Cloud HCM suite. The agents help HR leaders reshape the employee experience by automating routine tasks and providing intelligent recommendations. Oracle also expanded its B2C Service Center with Agent Suggestions powered by generative AI, allowing live agents to receive contextually relevant suggestions from multiple data sources including OCI Generative AI, Azure OpenAI, and custom REST endpoints.

The competition is intense. Salesforce, which announced Agentforce 2.0 in December 2024, issued a revenue forecast that fell short of analyst expectations, with projected revenue of $40.5 billion to $40.9 billion for fiscal year 2026 versus the $41.5 billion estimate. This tempered enthusiasm about AI agent returns, with CFO Dive reporting that Salesforce expects “modest” returns from its AI agent push this year.

Company Rollouts¶

Enterprises are deploying AI agents across diverse sectors, with concrete results emerging in healthcare, financial services, customer service, and logistics.

Healthcare¶

Innovaccer launched a suite of AI agents called “Agents of Care” designed to automate repetitive administrative tasks that contribute to clinician burnout. The agents handle scheduling, patient intake, referrals, and 24-hour support for patient inquiries. One agent conducts voice-activated protocol intake calls, asking patients questions about their emergency room visit and scheduling follow-ups. A referral agent connects patients with specialists and manages appointment logistics.

The rollout comes amid a projected shortage of 100,000 healthcare workers by 2028, according to consulting firm Mercer. Clinicians spend nearly nine hours per week on documentation alone, according to a Google Cloud study. Innovaccer, which recently raised $275 million in funding from investors including Generation Investment Management and Microsoft’s M12, is testing the agents at five health systems with general availability planned in two to three months.

Financial Services¶

BNY Mellon is managing “armies of AI agents” through its Eliza tool, an open-source system that enables multi-agent negotiations. The bank, America’s oldest, has deployed AI agents that can negotiate with each other to optimize internal processes. The system began development in 2023 and was released in 2024, with plans to upgrade to Eliza 2.0.

Goldman Sachs and Capital One are preparing for self-driving AI agents, according to American Banker. The financial institutions are exploring how agents can automate workflows while maintaining compliance in highly regulated environments. Routable added a fraud-detecting AI agent to its AP automation platform, identifying suspicious invoices and payment anomalies.

Customer Service¶

Cresta announced rapidly scaling AI voice agents in production, with strong results from deployments at Brinks Home Security. The AI Agent combines cutting-edge language models with business logic to deliver empathetic, human-like conversations that resolve complex issues. Brinks Home reported a 30-point increase in Net Promoter Score following the deployment.

Jotform launched AI Agents to transform customer service with a no-code solution. The platform enables organizations to deploy AI-powered virtual agents that manage inquiries and guide users through complex processes like booking, onboarding, and appointment scheduling. Jotform has over 30 million users worldwide .

HubSpot’s Agent.ai platform raced past 500,000 users after launching at INBOUND 2024. The platform, founded by HubSpot CTO Dharmesh Shah, grew from 250,000 users at the start of 2025, demonstrating rapid adoption. More than 5,000 people have used the low-code tool to build custom agents, and users have submitted 13,000 ratings and reviews with an average of 3.99 stars.

Real Estate¶

Christie’s is using AI agents to sell real estate and has generated $100 million in sales through the technology, according to Fox Business. The real estate giant’s Porta da Frente division has deployed agents to handle property transactions, demonstrating concrete ROI from AI agent adoption.

Telecom and Logistics¶

Deutsche Telekom partnered with Google Cloud on agentic AI for autonomous networks. The collaboration aims to develop AI agents that can manage network operations autonomously, improving efficiency and reducing manual intervention.

FourKites unveiled Tracy & Sam, two AI agents for supply chain efficiency. Tracy handles track and trace functions, automating repetitive tasks 24/7 and proactively contacting carriers to ensure data accuracy. Sam focuses on supplier collaboration, processing purchase orders and invoices. Early adopter Unilever reported improvements within four weeks of implementing Tracy, with the agent helping exception management and tracking quality. FourKites claims the Digital Workforce can increase shipment handling by 40% and automate up to 80% of routine tasks.

Developer Tools & Infrastructure¶

A new distribution model is emerging: AI agent marketplaces. Cloud platforms are also making it easier to build agents, and developer tools are adding agent capabilities.

Marketplaces & Stores¶

Olas launched the first-ever AI agent store with $13.8 million in backing. The decentralized marketplace enables developers to publish and monetize AI agents, similar to app stores. The funding raised by Olas aims to accelerate the development of an ecosystem where agents can be discovered, composed, and deployed.

Enso launched an AI agent marketplace in partnership with LangChain. The platform offers pre-built agents for business tasks, enabling organizations to deploy AI automation without building from scratch.

Cloud Platforms¶

Microsoft Azure announced Azure AI Agent Service in public preview, available through AI Foundry SDK and Portal. The service enables developers to build secure, stateful, autonomous AI agents in code-first environments. Microsoft also unveiled GPT-4.5, the latest general-purpose model, alongside specialized models including Phi-4, Phi-4-Vision, Phi-4-Coding, and Phi-4-Audio. The Azure AI Foundry model library now exceeds 1,800 offerings .

Amazon Web Services (AWS) introduced Amazon Q Developer Agents, streamlining development with AI-powered coding assistance. AWS also published guidance on building agentic AI solutions with DeepSeek-R1, CrewAI, and Amazon SageMaker AI.

Google Cloud announced tools to build scalable AI agents with Langbase and the Gemini API. The platform enables developers to create agents that leverage Gemini 2.0 for complex reasoning tasks.

Snowflake launched AI agents powered by Anthropic’s Claude, integrating the large language model into its data cloud platform. The agents can analyze data and generate insights within Snowflake’s infrastructure.

Zoho Corporation announced Zia Agents, a platform supporting autonomous agents across its portfolio of 100+ products. Zia Agents, Agent Studio, and Agent Marketplace empower enterprises to access, build, and distribute intelligent digital agents. The company, which onboarded 110,000 new customers in 2024, is previewing pre-built, task-specific agents that will deploy across its combined portfolio.

Developer Tools¶

GitHub Copilot unveiled “The agent awakens,” adding agent capabilities to the development platform. The upgrade enables Copilot to handle more complex coding workflows autonomously.

Postman introduced AI Agent Builder with three key features: visual agent design, testing, and deployment. The tool helps developers create agents that interact with APIs.

Docker added agent capabilities for containerized applications, enabling AI-driven operations within its platform.

Infrastructure Considerations¶

The infrastructure stack for AI agents is evolving. The New Stack reported on why AI agents need an operational database , highlighting the need for databases that can handle the state management, transaction consistency, and real-time requirements of agentic workloads.

Cisco’s Outshift group predicted that average-size organizations will deploy 20,000 AI agents , with enterprises handling hundreds of thousands. This has sparked discussion of an “Internet of Agents”—an open, interoperable framework for agent-to-agent communication.

Research and Innovation¶

Research institutions and companies are exploring novel applications of AI agents, from robotics to chip design.

Microsoft Research introduced Magma, an AI foundation model that combines visual and language processing to control both software interfaces and robotic systems. Unlike prior systems requiring separate models for perception and control, Magma integrates these abilities. The model scored 80.0 on the VQAv2 benchmark , higher than GPT-4V’s 77.2, and achieved 87.4 on the POPE benchmark. Microsoft plans to release the code publicly on GitHub.

NVIDIA introduced Marco, a multi-AI agent framework for chip design. The configurable graph-based system enables multiple agents to collaborate on complex chip design tasks, representing a novel application of agentic AI in semiconductor engineering.

University of Oulu researchers developed MAI (metacognitive AI agent), an AI tutor that taught physics to 12-13-year-old students in Finland. Unlike traditional AI applications that provide ready-made answers, MAI supports independent problem-solving by prompting students to think. The pilot found that students “appreciated MAI’s prompting feature” and that the agent helped group work dynamics.

Napkin AI launched a ‘design agency’ of AI agents that generates professional graphics from text prompts in under five seconds. The company doubled its user base to 2 million in six weeks. The platform uses a team of specialized AI agents—an orchestrator LLM assigns tasks to styling, rendering, and layout agents—demonstrating a vertical approach to agentic AI.

Logie AI launched “Influencer’s Agent Army” for affiliate marketing, automating influencer campaign management at scale.

Various researchers are exploring car-buying agents, AI agents that would handle vehicle research, pricing negotiation, and purchase. Computerworld reported on how an AI agent could help you buy your next car .

Microsoft published ExACT (Improving AI agents’ decision-making via test-time compute scaling), a research framework for enhancing agent performance through increased computational investment during task execution.

Challenges and Concerns¶

While AI agents are advancing rapidly, researchers and practitioners are raising important concerns about deployment, safety, and societal impact.

Researchers warned that fully autonomous AI agents “should not be deployed.” A paper reviewed in Process Excellence Network stated, “We find no clear benefit of fully autonomous AI agents, but many foreseeable harms from ceding full human control.” The researchers found that risks increase with system autonomy, particularly safety risks that affect human life. They recommended adoption of agent levels, human control mechanisms, and safety verification.

A Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon University study found that some workers risk becoming overly reliant on generative AI, with problem-solving skills declining as a result. The survey of 319 knowledge workers collected 936 examples of AI use, finding that higher confidence in AI correlates with less critical thinking.

Job displacement concerns are emerging. Microsoft executive Georg Glantschnig told CFO Dive that some finance jobs may “disappear” as AI agents automate labor-intensive processes like financial close. However, he noted that new roles like “agent business administrators” are emerging.

Security risks are multiplying. Malwarebytes reported on new AI agents that could hold people for ransom, representing an evolution of ransomware threats.

Business model questions persist. Reuters Breakingviews analysis noted that AI agents have a clear mission but hazy business models. Investors see vast potential—Ark Investments estimates AI agents could enable $9 trillion of global e-commerce sales by 2030—but monetization paths remain uncertain.

Confusion about what agents actually are persists. UC Today documented “AI Agent Confusion” , noting that the term ranges from simple chatbots to fully autonomous systems, making it difficult for enterprises to evaluate solutions.

UX concerns are growing. Built In identified three things needed before AI agents go mainstream: improved trust and transparency, better error handling, and seamless integration into existing workflows.

Privacy issues are emerging. SC Media outlined five privacy concerns around agentic AI, including data collection, surveillance, and the potential for agents to expose sensitive information.

Responsible AI is becoming a priority. IBM published “The Rise of Responsible AI Agents” , emphasizing the need for ethical development practices.

Legal perspectives are emerging. Above the Law published guidance for in-house counsel on agents versus agentic AI, highlighting legal considerations for enterprise adoption.

Implementation challenges are significant. A Constellation Research report noted that AI agents bring new consumption models to SaaS, asking whether this represents a “Goldilocks or headache” for enterprises managing software costs.

Funding and Startups¶

Startup activity and funding rounds reflect growing investor interest in AI agent infrastructure and applications.

Olas raised $13.8 million to launch its decentralized AI agent store, according to VentureBeat . The funding aims to build a marketplace where agents can be discovered, composed, and deployed.

Qeen.ai, an AI agents for e-commerce startup founded by Google and DeepMind alumni, raised a $10 million seed round . The company, which employs 50 people, offers autonomous agents for e-commerce automation.

Pinkfish emerged from stealth with a $7.6 million pre-seed round led by Norwest Venture Partners. The startup helps enterprises build AI agents through natural language processing, with more than 200 integrations including Salesforce and Zendesk. Customers include Ipsy, Elevate, and Talkdesk.

SoftBank and OpenAI partnered to push AGI and agents, according to Axios . The partnership aims to accelerate the development of artificial general intelligence with Japanese focus.

Nvidia’s revenue is surging from agentic AI demand. CEO Jensen Huang reported that Blackwell GPU architecture booked $11 billion in Q4 revenue, with data center revenues more than doubling. The company sees agentic AI as the next stage after generative AI, with “physical AI” like robots following after.

India accounts for 40% of AI agent deployments globally for Automation Anywhere, driven by global capability centers and large enterprises. The company reported two-fold growth in AI agent deals and an 80% success rate from proof-of-concept to production.

Looking Ahead¶

Three distinct trends emerge from February’s developments:

First, AI agents are moving from prototypes to production. OpenAI’s Operator and Google’s AI co-scientist represent productized agents available to users, not just research demonstrations. The focus is shifting from “what can agents do?” to “how do we deploy and manage them at scale?”

Second, enterprises are building agent management systems. Workday’s Agent System of Record, ServiceNow’s AI Agent Orchestrator, and SAP’s Joule represent infrastructure for treating AI agents as digital employees. This reflects the recognition that managing fleets of agents requires new categories of enterprise software.

Third, new distribution channels are emerging. Agent marketplaces from Olas and Enso represent a new model for discovering and deploying AI agents. This could accelerate adoption by making agents easier to find and integrate.

The corporate venturing community is actively investing. Global Corporate Venturing reported that companies are rushing to invest in AI agent startups, sensing both opportunity and competitive threat.

Looking at a 10-year horizon, NFX predicted the rise of an “AI agent economy” , where agent marketplaces become the primary distribution channel for software and services.

However, expectations are outpacing reality. IBM’s analysis of “AI Agents in 2025: Expectations vs. Reality” noted that while excitement is high, many deployments remain in pilot or experimentation phases. Salesforce’s tempered revenue forecast reinforces that AI agent adoption will be gradual rather than explosive.

The workplace of the future will include both humans and AI agents. As Axios noted , workplace chat will feature more AI agents and fewer humans over time. The question is not whether AI agents will enter the workforce, but how quickly enterprises will adapt their management practices, security protocols, and workforce strategies to accommodate them.

2025 is shaping up to be the year of enterprise experimentation with AI agents—testing use cases, building management infrastructure, and learning what works. The transition to widespread production deployments will take longer, but the foundation is being laid now.
