Researchers demonstrated autonomous AI systems that spontaneously share knowledge to accelerate materials discovery. Meanwhile, scientists developed rare-earth-free magnets using abundant elements, and new AI tools are making molecular design 10x faster.

AI Systems Learn to Collaborate¶

A joint research team from NIMS and the University of Tsukuba developed autonomous AI network technology that enables multiple AI systems to spontaneously collaborate by sharing knowledge for materials discovery. In simulations, three AI systems exchanged knowledge and improved optimization speed through this spontaneous collaboration.

The technology mimics how human researchers communicate and share insights across labs. Rather than working in isolation, the AI systems transfer learned knowledge to each other, accelerating the exploration of materials space. The team published their findings in npj Computational Materials in December 2025.

This approach addresses a key bottleneck in computational materials discovery: each AI system typically starts from scratch, even when similar problems have been solved elsewhere. By enabling knowledge transfer between autonomous systems, the technology could accelerate the discovery and synthesis of new materials across distributed research networks.

Materials Breakthroughs¶

Rare-Earth-Free Magnets¶

Researchers at Georgetown University discovered a new class of strong magnets based on high-entropy borides that use only earth-abundant transition metals and boron. The materials exhibit high magnetic anisotropy , a key property for permanent magnets, without requiring rare-earth elements.

The team synthesized the materials using a combinatorial sputtering method that enables rapid testing of multiple compositions simultaneously—up to 50 samples on a single substrate. The high-entropy approach combines five or more elements in near-equal proportions, creating materials with properties distinct from conventional alloys.

This work opens pathways toward sustainable magnetic technologies for motors, MRI machines, and data storage. The research was published in Advanced Materials in 2025.

Robust Crystalline Sponge¶

The Institute of Science Tokyo developed APF-80 , a robust metal-organic framework (MOF) for the crystalline sponge method used in molecular structure analysis. Previous MOFs were too fragile for practical use, limiting the technique’s application.

APF-80 overcomes these fragility issues , enabling structural elucidation of complex, reactive molecules like alkaloids from minute quantities. The method works by absorbing target molecules into the MOF’s pores, where they can be analyzed via X-ray diffraction without requiring crystallization of the molecule itself.

This advancement is expected to accelerate pharmaceutical research by enabling structure determination of compounds that are difficult to crystallize or available only in small amounts.

New Quantum State¶

Physicists from Rice University and Vienna University of Technology discovered a topological semimetal phase in CeRu4Sn6 at near absolute zero temperature. The state emerged through quantum criticality and was identified via an unusual Hall effect without an external magnetic field.

The finding challenges previous assumptions: strong electron interactions can create, not destroy, topological states . This insight could inform advances in quantum computing, electronic efficiencies, and sensing technologies by revealing new mechanisms for engineering topological quantum materials.

AI Tools Accelerate Discovery¶

Faster Molecular Design¶

Researchers from NYU and the University of Florida developed PropMolFlow , an AI model that designs molecules with specified properties 10 times faster than previous methods. The system reduces computational steps from 1,000 to 100 while maintaining 90% accuracy for generating chemically valid structures.

The method works backward from desired properties to molecular structures, validated using physics-based density functional theory. This “inverse design” approach accelerates discovery for pharmaceuticals and materials by directly targeting specific characteristics rather than screening large libraries. The work was published in Nature Computational Science in January 2026.

Human-AI Collaboration¶

Argonne National Laboratory and the University of Chicago developed an AI advisor model for autonomous labs that enables collaborative decision-making between humans and AI. Applied to the Polybot self-driving lab, the system created a mixed ion-electron conducting polymer with a 150% performance improvement over previous methods.

The approach combines human expertise with AI’s data-processing capabilities, allowing researchers to guide autonomous experiments while leveraging machine learning for optimization. The team published their findings in Nature Chemical Engineering in December 2025.

Quantum Machine Learning¶

Researchers from the University of Maryland and IonQ demonstrated that quantum kernel machine learning outperforms classical methods in navigating materials phase space using less experimental data. The study, conducted on IonQ’s Aria trapped-ion quantum computer, used X-ray diffraction patterns from an Fe-Ga-Pd alloy library.

The work provides experimental evidence for quantum advantage in materials discovery, particularly when working with complex diffraction data and limited experimental samples . The findings suggest quantum computing could accelerate autonomous experimentation in materials science.

Data Infrastructure¶

NIMS developed Research Data Express (RDE) , a flexible data management system designed to standardize and automate materials science data processing. Since its 2023 launch, the platform has seen significant adoption: 5,000 users , 1,900 Dataset Templates implemented, 16,000 datasets created, and 3 million accumulated data files .

RDE uses customizable Dataset Templates to interpret data from various experiments, reducing researchers’ data processing burden and promoting FAIR data principles. The system serves as key infrastructure for Japan’s national materials science initiatives, addressing the critical bottleneck of inconsistent and siloed data that hampers collaborative research and AI-driven discovery.

Funding and Industry Moves¶

TU/e secured $1.5 million euros as part of the $6 million euro Horizon Europe project SimuLingua, which aims to develop a next-generation AI platform for materials discovery. The project will create a multimodal scientific foundation model enabling researchers to explore materials via natural language and AI-accelerated simulations.

Novyte Materials raised pre-seed funding led by Theia Ventures to support its AI-driven materials discovery platform. The company aims to cut R&D time by up to 10x and reduce testing costs by 90%.

South Korea’s Ministry of Science and ICT announced a $275.4 billion won investment in the 2026 Nano and Materials R&D Project, focusing on securing core materials technologies and integrating AI to accelerate research.

Energy Fuels is acquiring Australian Strategic Materials for approximately $447 million AUD, highlighting strategic moves to secure critical minerals supply chains and reduce dependence on China for rare earth processing.

Also This Week¶

Pablo Jarillo-Herrero (MIT) and Allan MacDonald (University of Texas) won the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award for their discovery of the “magic angle” in graphene layers. Their work on twistronics—rotating graphene at 1.1 degrees to control electronic properties—has applications in quantum computing and energy-efficient AI.

The Materials Project has been cited 32,000 times and serves 650,000 users, accelerating research in batteries and quantum computing. The platform provides high-throughput computational modeling and AI-ready datasets, with 99.98% uptime on its cloud infrastructure.
