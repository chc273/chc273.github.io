This month brought a coordinated surge in quantum hardware announcements. IBM unveiled two new processors targeting fault tolerance by 2029. Quantinuum launched Helios, which it calls the most accurate quantum computer. IQM introduced the Halocene line for error correction research. Behind these product releases, researchers achieved breakthroughs in qubit coherence, quantum networking, and error correction that could fundamentally change the scaling trajectory.

This comprehensive review covers 88 articles published in November 2025, surveying the latest developments in quantum computing.

The Hardware Wave¶

The most visible story this month was a coordinated wave of quantum processor announcements from leading hardware companies, all converging on error correction as the priority.

IBM unveiled two new quantum processors in November. Nighthawk features 120 qubits with 218 tunable couplers —20% more than the previous Heron processor—enabling 30% more complex calculations . The chip supports up to 5,000 two-qubit gates , with iterations planned to reach 7,500 gates by end of 2026 and 15,000 gates by 2028 .

The second processor, Loon, incorporates hardware designed specifically for fault tolerance , including low-loss routing layers and qubit reset capabilities . IBM’s error decoding achieves 480-nanosecond latency . The company targets quantum advantage by 2026 and fully fault-tolerant quantum computing by 2029 , with a planned 2,000-qubit Starling processor for that year. Both processors use 300mm wafer fabrication , which halves manufacturing time and enables a 10-times increase in physical complexity .

Quantinuum launched Helios, a system the company calls “easily the most powerful quantum computer on Earth” . The system features 98 physical qubits achieving 48 fully error-corrected logical qubits at a 2:1 physical-to-logical ratio —significantly better than the 10:1 ratio commonly assumed. The system achieved 99.921% two-qubit gate fidelity across all qubit pairs and 99.9975% single-qubit gate fidelity . Helios uses redesigned junction ion-trap hardware with barium ions and a new software stack featuring the Guppy programming language . Enterprise partners including SoftBank , JPMorgan Chase , and Google used the system during early access for finance and materials science applications. The system was used to simulate a superconducting metal , revealing new electron behavior.

IQM Quantum Computers launched the Halocene product line, designed specifically for quantum error correction research . The first release is a 150-qubit system with advanced error correction features, scheduled for commercial availability by end of 2026 . The system supports research on up to five logical qubits and incorporates the company’s IQM Crystal QPU with 99.7% physical two-qubit gate fidelity . Future product releases target qubit counts beyond 1,000 .

China’s Hanyuan-1 quantum computer achieved commercialization in November, with orders worth over 40 million yuan ($5.6 million) including an export order to Pakistan . The system features 100 qubits with 99.9% single-qubit gate fidelity and 98% two-qubit gate fidelity . Notably, it operates without cryogenic refrigeration , reducing maintenance and energy consumption by over 90% compared to superconducting systems . The entire machine fits into three standard racks . The system has been integrated with the Atomic Quantum Computing Cloud Platform , connecting 50 universities and enterprises . The team aims to provide large-scale computing services by 2027 .

Other November hardware announcements included IonQ announcing a record-low error rate above 99.99% for two-qubit gates and a method to skip cooling steps to improve speed , and Oxford Ionics introducing a multicore approach using trapped ions . D-Wave made its Advantage2 system available to U.S. government customers , adding another option for federal agencies exploring quantum applications.

Under the Hood: Error Correction Advances¶

While hardware announcements grabbed headlines, the underlying research advances may prove more significant for the field’s trajectory. November brought breakthroughs in qubit coherence, magic state distillation, and fault-tolerant architectures.

Coherence Breakthrough¶

Princeton University engineers published work on a new superconducting qubit achieving over 1 millisecond of coherence time —three times longer than the previous lab record and nearly 15 times longer than the industry standard . The design uses tantalum on a silicon substrate and is compatible with existing processors like Google’s . The researchers project the design could improve system reliability by up to 1,000 times for Google’s Willow processor and by 1 billion times for a hypothetical 1,000-qubit computer .

The advance addresses the core bottleneck in error correction: longer coherence means fewer errors occur during computation, reducing the overhead for correction. The work represents the largest single advance in qubit coherence time in over a decade . The paper was published in Nature .

Optimizing Distillation¶

Researchers from MIT’s Center for Theoretical Physics achieved theoretically optimal scaling for magic state distillation , reaching a scaling exponent of zero —meaning constant overhead. This resolves a long-standing theoretical problem by proving that the number of noisy input states needed per high-quality output state does not have to grow as error rates decrease. The previous best scaling, achieved in 2017, was approximately 0.678 .

The work, published in Nature Physics , bridges a gap between theoretical qudit systems and practical qubit-based quantum computers. Magic state distillation is essential for implementing non-Clifford gates (such as T gates), which are required for universal quantum computation but cannot be performed directly with error-corrected Clifford operations. Constant overhead means significantly fewer physical resources are needed for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Fault-Tolerant Architectures¶

Researchers published experimental work on a fault-tolerant neutral-atom architecture using reconfigurable arrays of up to 448 neutral atoms . They demonstrated below-threshold error correction with surface codes, showing 2.14x lower error per round for a distance-5 code compared to distance-3. The work demonstrated logical entanglement via transversal gates and achieved a two-orders-of-magnitude increase in cycle rates through mid-circuit qubit reuse. The team achieved 0.46% bit-flip error rate and 0.24% loss rate in non-destructive readout. With loss information and machine learning decoding, they achieved 1.73x better QEC performance compared to conventional methods.

Separate work proposed a fiber array architecture for atom quantum computing that enables fully independent control of individual atoms . Researchers experimentally demonstrated trapping and independent control of ten single atoms , achieving 99.66% average fidelity for individually addressed single-qubit gates and 99.61% for simultaneous arbitrary single-qubit gates on four qubits . This approach enables high-rate, highly parallel gate operations, which could accelerate quantum algorithm execution on neutral atom systems.

Connecting the Quantum Internet¶

University of Chicago researchers extended the maximum distance for quantum computer connections to 2,000 kilometers —a 200X improvement over the previous record of approximately 10 km. The breakthrough uses molecular-beam epitaxy to create high-purity rare-earth doped crystals , which enhance coherence from 0.1 milliseconds to over 10 milliseconds . In one instance, the team demonstrated 24 milliseconds of coherence , which would enable 4,000 km theoretical connection distance . The research was published in Nature Communications .

Separately, University of Chicago scientists built a molecular qubit using erbium that transmits quantum information at telecom wavelengths , making it compatible with existing fiber-optic networks . The qubit acts as a bridge between magnetism and optics, storing information magnetically and being read out optically. The molecular qubit is 100,000 times smaller than a human hair , enabling integration with silicon chips. This research was published in Science .

Google Quantum AI published a five-stage roadmap to guide quantum computing research from abstract theory to practical applications. The framework highlights a critical bottleneck in demonstrating quantum advantage for real-world problems (Stage III) and calls for cultural and funding shifts to support cross-disciplinary work. The team proposes using AI tools to help map known quantum algorithmic advantages to concrete application areas.

Quantum at Work: Early Applications¶

Simulation Milestones¶

A team from the Jülich Supercomputing Center and NVIDIA achieved the first full simulation of a 50-qubit universal quantum computer —surpassing the previous record of 48 qubits set in 2022 . The simulation ran on Europe’s first exascale supercomputer, JUPITER , using more than 16,000 NVIDIA GH200 Superchips and requiring approximately 2 petabytes of memory . This enables high-fidelity testing of quantum algorithms such as VQE and QAOA for chemistry, materials science, and optimization applications.

U.S. Department of Energy researchers performed one of the largest digital quantum simulations on IBM quantum hardware, using scalable quantum circuits to simulate a one-dimensional quantum electrodynamics model with over 100 qubits . They achieved percent-level accuracy in simulating vacuum properties and preparing hadron pulses, enabling simulations of matter under extreme conditions beyond classical computation.

A U.S.-Canadian research team used a trapped ion quantum computer to simulate a simplified one-dimensional phase diagram of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) . This work could provide a foundation for understanding extreme environments like the inside of neutron stars or the early universe, which are intractable for classical computers. The team introduced a new type of ancillae register to make quantum computations more efficient.

Photonic Advances¶

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) experimentally realized universal quantum gates and generated a six-qubit entangled state using a photonic quantum walk system. They achieved this by encoding three qubits onto a single photon via polarization and path encoding, then entangling two such photons. This deterministic approach using linear optics advances the potential for scalable, room-temperature photonic quantum computing.

KAIST researchers developed an efficient Multimode Quantum Process Tomography technique for optical quantum computers, reducing the data needed to characterize complex quantum operations. They achieved the first experimental characterization of a 16-mode optical quantum process , overcoming previous limitations.

Practical Tests¶

D-Wave demonstrated significant advantages over classical supercomputers , claiming to solve complex simulations in minutes instead of nearly a million years with minimal energy consumption. The company reported electricity costs of less than $1 for simulations that would require as much electricity as the world uses in a year on a classical supercomputer. These capabilities are being applied through partnerships with organizations like JSC and Japan Tobacco .

Haiqu, a quantum software company, demonstrated that quantum computers can detect anomalies more efficiently than classical systems using an IBM Quantum Heron processor. Their proprietary quantum encoding technique loaded over 500 features into quantum circuits on 128 qubits , showing potential for quantum advantage in machine learning for finance and healthcare applications.

Speculative Ideas¶

Researchers from King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) proposed using blimps to lift quantum data centers to the stratosphere , where the natural cold reduces the energy needed to cool qubits. The floating platforms, called QC-HAPs , would be powered by solar energy and communicate via light beams. At 20 km altitude where temperatures reach -50 degrees C , the concept could achieve 21% energy cost savings for ion trap quantum computers. This remains a proposal rather than an active development program.

The Partnership Network¶

North America¶

IBM and Cisco announced a collaboration to develop networked distributed quantum computing , targeting an initial proof-of-concept by 2030 . The partnership aims to link large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computers with a long-term vision of enabling a quantum computing internet by the late 2030s . They plan demonstrations of multiple connected QPUs within the next three years .

California Governor Gavin Newsom announced Quantum California, a statewide initiative backed by $4 million in funding to strengthen California’s leadership in quantum technology. The initiative aligns UC Berkeley, UC San Diego, UCLA, Stanford, and Caltech with industry leaders including Google, Microsoft, and Amazon .

PsiQuantum selected Jacobs as the Owner’s Representative for a new quantum computing facility in Chicago . The project involves building an intermediate-scale test system and progressing toward a utility-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computer. Construction has begun on the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park (IQMP) , a 128-acre campus with the first phase occupying 30 acres and scheduled for completion in 2027 .

PsiQuantum and Lockheed Martin expanded their partnership to apply quantum computing to complex optimization problems, including the “cocktail party problem” of separating overlapping signals in defense applications.

IonQ announced a partnership with the University of Chicago to advance quantum workforce development and research, including access to IonQ systems for academic research.

Microsoft announced expansion of its quantum operations in Denmark , investing in facilities and talent to advance topological qubit development. The company also opened a new quantum lab in Chicago , expanding its physical research presence.

Europe¶

The European Space Agency (ESA) selected Equal1 to install a Hybrid Quantum Computing System for Earth Observation , deploying Equal1’s Bell-1 Quantum Computer to accelerate processing of complex Earth Observation data. The 6-qubit system consumes 1,600 W and is designed for seamless integration with ESA’s existing HPC infrastructure.

SDT Inc. joined the QuEra Quantum Alliance to advance the commercialization and industrial adoption of neutral-atom quantum computing . SDT, as South Korea’s only full-stack quantum company, will contribute its Quantum Design & Manufacturing (QDM) framework to accelerate practical deployment. QuEra recently installed a $41 million neutral-atom quantum computer at Japan’s AIST.

SemiQon and Conductor Technology announced a partnership to accelerate the manufacture of silicon-based quantum processors using semiconductor industry techniques.

Alice & Bob and Thales expanded their collaboration to develop error-corrected quantum solutions for aerospace and defense applications.

Asia-Pacific & Middle East¶

NTT and OptQC signed a collaboration agreement to develop scalable optical quantum computers with a goal of achieving one million qubits by 2030 . The partnership combines NTT’s optical communication technologies with OptQC’s expertise in building room-temperature quantum systems. OptQC is currently developing a 10,000-qubit system with its first commercial optical quantum computer scheduled for completion in April 2026 . NTT’s optical amplification technology achieves quantum entanglement generation rates 1,000 times faster than conventional methods.

Saudi Arabia is establishing its first quantum computing capabilities through a partnership between Saudi Aramco and Pasqal. The initiative will deploy Pasqal’s neutral-atom quantum computers for energy industry applications, making Saudi Arabia the first country in the Middle East with commercial quantum computing access.

Qilimanjaro announced expansion into the Asia-Pacific market with a new headquarters in Singapore.

Market Dynamics¶

The industry’s underlying economics shifted in November, with one major IPO and a sobering assessment of manufacturing challenges.

Xanadu Quantum Technologies announced it will go public via a business combination with Crane Harbor Acquisition Corp. , valuing the company at $3.6 billion . This makes it the first publicly traded pure-play photonic quantum computing company . The transaction includes $500 million in gross proceeds from the business combination and $275 million from a PIPE offering . Xanadu’s achievements include quantum supremacy with its 216-qubit Borealis system , which completed a task in 2 minutes that would take a classical supercomputer 7 million years . The company’s PennyLane software holds 47% usage share among quantum programmers.

Rigetti Computing reported Q3 2025 financial results showing sequential revenue growth of 5.6% but a year-over-year decline of 20.6% . The company unveiled an aggressive roadmap targeting 1,000+ qubits by 2027 . Rigetti reported strong liquidity with $558.9 million in cash and investments as of quarter-end, increasing to approximately $600 million by November 6, 2025 .

John Martinis, the Google quantum computing pioneer, published a critique arguing that quantum computing progress is stalled due to manufacturing limitations . He emphasized the need to shift from hand-built lab devices to integrated, factory-produced chips to reach the million-qubit scale needed for general-purpose machines. Martinis noted that Google’s quantum chips grew from 53 qubits to 105 qubits over six years —doubling rather than exponential growth. He called for abandoning outdated techniques like the “lift-off” process and developing cryogenic integrated circuits . Without this manufacturing overhaul, he warned, the field risks remaining “trapped in a jungle of wires” and decades away from practical systems .

Microsoft announced major investments in quantum facilities , including expansion in Denmark for topological qubit development and a new quantum lab in Chicago.

J.P. Morgan reported that global quantum venture investment increased 17x over the past decade , with significant acceleration in 2025. The bank identified error correction and fault tolerance as key thresholds for practical applications.

Beyond Hardware¶

Software & Access¶

Kvantify launched Qrunch, a quantum computing platform designed for chemical and biological engineering . The platform abstracts quantum complexity into an intuitive interface, allowing scientists to build quantum algorithms without quantum expertise. Qrunch has demonstrated up to 80 qubit simulations with 3-4x improvement in problem-size capacity . The platform integrates with gate-based quantum computers including superconducting systems via Amazon Braket and IQM systems .

QSolid made its prototype quantum computer available for first external testing , providing researchers with access to superconducting quantum hardware for experimental validation.

Policy & Security¶

Quantum computing was actively showcased at COP30 with the release of a sustainability report by the Sustainability Quantum Working Group, which includes 60+ experts . The report demonstrates how quantum systems are addressing concrete problems in energy, materials, and climate modeling.

The UK government’s Critical Minerals Strategy 2035 aims to secure critical minerals for high-tech industries by boosting domestic production, midstream processing, and recycling. The strategy targets 20% of UK mineral demand to be met via recycled materials by 2035 and includes a 50 million pound funding program . Quantum technologies, which depend on materials like rare earths and ultra-pure metals, will benefit from reduced supply-chain vulnerabilities.

Computer scientists are developing post-quantum cryptography using mathematical problems such as structured lattices and hash functions to secure data against future quantum attacks. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is considering four problems for post-quantum cryptography standards. Experts emphasize the importance of cryptographic agility to protect against future quantum threats.

Industry Reflections¶

Nature Materials interviewed Pascale Senellart-Mardon, co-founder of Quandela, about photonic quantum computing technologies and the challenges of juggling academia and industry.

Several publications published retrospectives on 2025 quantum breakthroughs , noting the shift from pure research to early deployment with commercial systems like Quantinuum’s Helios and demonstrations of quantum advantage. The year saw quantum computing equity funding reach $3.77 billion in the first nine months of 2025, compared to $1.3 billion in all of 2024 . National government investment reached $10 billion by April 2025 , compared to $1.8 billion in all of 2024 .

Network World reported that enterprise adoption is accelerating, with 60% of business leaders actively investing in or exploring quantum AI . A D-Wave survey found that 81% of leaders said they’ve reached the limit with classical computers , and 53% are planning to build quantum computing into workflows . Quantum computing could potentially unlock $250 billion in market value across pharma, finance, logistics, and materials science.

The Road to 2029¶

November’s developments reveal quantum computing at a transition point. The hardware announcements represent real systems with specified availability dates, not theoretical roadmaps. IBM’s Nighthawk and Loon processors are available now. Quantinuum’s Helios is commercially deployed. IQM’s Halocene has a 2026 commercial availability target. These are engineering milestones, not physics experiments.

The error correction advances are equally substantive. Princeton’s 1ms qubit directly addresses the coherence bottleneck that determines how many operations can be performed before errors accumulate. The optimal magic state distillation scaling resolves a theoretical problem that has constrained fault-tolerant architectures for two decades. The neutral-atom fault-tolerant architecture demonstrates below-threshold error correction with surface codes—the gold standard for quantum error correction.

But John Martinis’s manufacturing critique is the necessary reality check. His observation that Google’s chips grew from 53 to 105 qubits over six years—roughly doubling, not exponential—contradicts narratives of rapid scaling. His argument that the field needs to transition from hand-built lab devices to factory-produced chips resonates because IBM’s 300mm wafer fabrication announcement represents exactly that type of manufacturing advance.

The quantum networking breakthrough extends the potential scale of quantum systems beyond single machines. University of Chicago’s 2,000 km connection distance and the erbium molecular qubit’s compatibility with existing fiber networks mean quantum computers could eventually be networked globally. IBM and Cisco’s collaboration to build networked distributed quantum computing by 2030 is a direct response to this possibility.

The field is transitioning from physics experiments to engineering challenges. The 2029 fault-tolerance target that IBM, Quantinuum, and others are coalescing around is aggressive but—for the first time—backed by coherent technical plans rather than speculative roadmaps. The question is whether manufacturing can scale fast enough to realize those plans.

The next three years will determine whether 2029 marks the beginning of the fault-tolerant era or another milestone on a longer timeline. What November made clear is that the path forward requires advances on three fronts simultaneously: better qubits (Princeton’s 1ms coherence), better error correction (optimal magic state distillation, below-threshold surface codes), and better manufacturing (IBM’s 300mm wafers, Martinis’s cryogenic integrated circuits). All three showed progress this month.
