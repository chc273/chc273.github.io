OpenAI released Operator, a research preview of an AI agent that browses the web and performs tasks autonomously. The launch signals a shift from chatbots to agents, with enterprises racing to adopt agentic AI while researchers tackle reliability and safety challenges.

This comprehensive review covers 118 articles published between January and February 2025, surveying the rapid evolution of AI agents from research curiosities to enterprise products.

The Lead: OpenAI’s Operator Enters the Arena¶

OpenAI has released Operator, a research preview of an AI agent that uses the Computer-Using Agent (CUA) model to control a browser through a visual interface. Rather than relying on website APIs, Operator interacts with web pages the way a human does—by clicking buttons, typing in forms, and navigating menus—allowing it to perform tasks like booking travel, ordering groceries, and filling out forms.

The performance benchmarks are notable but reveal the limitations of current technology. Operator achieves an 87% success rate on the WebVoyager benchmark, which measures real website navigation capabilities. On WebArena, which tests ecommerce and content management scenarios, it reaches 58.1%. However, on the OSWorld benchmark for general computer tasks, Operator scores 38.1% compared to 72.4% for human performance, highlighting the gap remaining before agents match human reliability across diverse tasks.

Operator is available initially to ChatGPT Pro subscribers at $200 per month, with OpenAI planning to expand availability to other tiers later. The research preview is limited to the United States and not available in Europe at launch due to regulatory considerations.

OpenAI has partnered with major platforms for testing, including eBay , Instacart , and DoorDash , to explore how the agent can handle real-world tasks like grocery shopping and food delivery.

Early reviews indicate that Operator still requires significant supervision. Bloomberg’s testing found the agent needs “adult supervision” for complex tasks, particularly when unexpected situations arise. The research preview designation acknowledges these limitations—this is an early look at agentic capabilities, not a finished product ready for unsupervised operation.

Operator’s launch is part of a broader industry trend. Google announced Project Mariner in December 2024, and Anthropic introduced “Computer Use” in October 2024. What sets OpenAI’s entry apart is the scale of its distribution through the existing ChatGPT user base and the company’s positioning of 2025 as “the year of agentic AI.”

Platform Wars: The Agent Builder Eruption¶

Operator isn’t alone in the market. A wave of platform announcements from enterprise giants demonstrates how agentic AI has become a competitive battleground across the software industry.

Enterprise Platforms¶

ServiceNow delivered what may be the most comprehensive enterprise agent announcement of the period. The company introduced thousands of pre-configured AI agents , a no-code AI Agent Studio for building custom agents, and an AI Agent Orchestrator for managing multi-step workflows across enterprise systems. The company reports nearly 1,000 customers have already begun their agentic AI journey, with AI Agent Studio and Orchestrator becoming generally available in March 2025 .

ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott forecasts that AI agents will save workers a day a week this year (a 20% productivity boost) and 2.5 days a week next year (a 50% boost), creating what he calls a “massive productivity dividend.” To support this vision, ServiceNow is acquiring conversational AI platform Cuein to enhance agents’ ability to analyze conversation data.

NVIDIA announced the Nemotron model families , including Llama Nemotron LLMs and Cosmos Nemotron VLMs, designed as foundation models for enterprise agentic AI. Built in collaboration with Meta, these models come in Nano, Super, and Ultra sizes and are optimized for tasks like instruction following, coding, and visual analysis. The models are available as NVIDIA NIM microservices for easy deployment.

Cohere launched North, an AI workspace platform optimized for air-gapped environments , positioning it as an enterprise-focused alternative to cloud-dependent agent platforms. The company partnered with Royal Bank of Canada as an early customer, reflecting financial services’ interest in AI solutions that can operate securely within regulated environments.

Developer Tools¶

JetBrains released Junie , an AI coding agent integrated directly into JetBrains IDEs. Junie achieves a 53.6% score on the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark, a measure of software engineering capability. The agent can handle code generation, debugging, and refactoring tasks within the development environment.

Postman added an AI agent builder combining LLMs with its API platform, allowing developers to create agents that can interact with APIs through natural language. This bridges the gap between API development and agentic AI, enabling agents to programmatically access services through Postman’s extensive API network.

Block (Jack Dorsey’s company) open-sourced Goose under the Apache 2.0 license, providing a framework for building AI agents that can be redirected mid-task—a critical capability for real-world applications where requirements change. The open-source approach contrasts with closed platforms and aims to accelerate experimentation.

Evaluation & Infrastructure¶

The ecosystem is maturing beyond basic agent creation. Galileo launched Agentic Evaluations , a platform for testing agent performance across multiple dimensions. Microsoft introduced pay-as-you-go access for AI agents with M365 Copilot Chat, addressing cost concerns by letting enterprises pay only for what they use.

Palo Alto Networks announced AI Runtime Security for protecting agents during execution, addressing growing security concerns as agents become more autonomous. NVIDIA released guardrails and tools specifically for enterprise agent deployment, including NeMo Guardrails and NIM microservices for safe production use.

Enterprise Reality Check: Enthusiasm Meets Integration Challenges¶

The enthusiasm for AI agents is real, but implementation is proving difficult. A comprehensive survey by eMarketer found that 93% of IT leaders are implementing or planning to implement AI agents within two years, but 80% cite data integration as a major obstacle. The average enterprise uses 897 apps, with only 29% interconnected, creating a fragmented data landscape that hinders agent effectiveness.

The UiPath 2025 Agentic AI Report, surveying 252 U.S. IT executives, reveals both optimism and practical barriers:

  • 90% have business processes that would be improved by agentic AI
  • 77% are prepared to invest in agentic AI this year
  • 56% identify IT security as a top concern
  • 35% cite integration with existing systems as a concern
  • 37% identify cost of implementation as a concern

A BCG survey of 1,803 executives found that one-third of companies plan to spend over $25 million on AI in 2025, reflecting significant investment commitment. However, the survey also identified a critical “ambition-results gap”: only 25% of executives report meaningful value from their AI initiatives. The survey found that leading companies focus AI investments on reshaping core functions (80% of investments) rather than just productivity gains (56% for other companies).

Real-world deployments are beginning to show concrete results:

  • Fujitsu achieved a 67% productivity boost by implementing Azure AI Agent Service to automate sales proposal creation, enabling around 38,000 employees to focus on customer engagement rather than document preparation
  • Baca Systems cut customer service response times from 5-7 minutes to 5-10 seconds using Salesforce agents, transforming the customer experience
  • Boost.ai and Sage deployed an accounting AI agent in just 50 days, demonstrating rapid implementation timelines
  • BNY has deployed 40 AI production use cases and made the platform available to all employees

Microsoft’s IT division, Microsoft Digital, is fundamentally restructuring around AI agents as “digital colleagues,” treating agents as core organizational components rather than optional tools. The company has also deployed employee-built agents for network infrastructure monitoring, demonstrating how organizations can extend AI platforms for internal operational tasks.

Research: Making Agents More Capable and Reliable¶

Agent reliability is improving through targeted research addressing core limitations in generalization, robustness, domain-specific reasoning, and security.

Researchers from Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications developed AgentRefine, which uses a Dungeons & Dragons-inspired framework to teach AI agents self-correction. The approach improves agents’ ability to generalize and perform better on unfamiliar tasks and in new environments. Tested on models including GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, and Llama variants, AgentRefine enhanced adaptability and reduced errors, addressing a key limitation in current agent deployments.

MIT researchers discovered a counterintuitive finding they call the “indoor training effect”: training AI agents in noise-free environments actually leads to better performance in noisy conditions than training directly in noisy environments. The research, demonstrated using modified Atari games, challenges the conventional wisdom of matching training and testing environments. This finding could inform better training methods for AI agents deployed in uncertain real-world scenarios.

Pacific Northwest National Laboratory created CACTUS, an AI agent that connects LLM reasoning with cheminformatics tools for drug discovery. CACTUS can predict molecular properties, assess drug-likeness, and facilitate autonomous experimentation. The system runs on both consumer-grade hardware and supercomputers, democratizing access to computational chemistry tools and accelerating scientific research.

AMD and Johns Hopkins University built Agent Laboratory, a framework that automates scientific research processes including literature reviews, experimentation, and report writing. The system demonstrated an 84% reduction in research costs compared to existing autonomous methods while maintaining quality standards, with workflow costs dropping from $13.10 for o1-preview to $2.33 for gpt-4o.

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) conducted agent hijacking evaluations using the AgentDojo framework. Working with the UK AI Security Institute, they found that optimized attacks could increase success rates from 11% to 81% on upgraded Claude 3.5 Sonnet. This research highlights the importance of continuous evaluation and improvement of security frameworks to mitigate hijacking risks, particularly for attacks like indirect prompt injection, remote code execution, and automated phishing.

These research advances address critical barriers to agent adoption: generalization beyond training data, robustness in uncertain conditions, domain-specific expertise, and security against hijacking. The focus is shifting from “can agents work?” to “how do we make agents reliable enough for production deployment?”

Funding: Vertical AI Agents Attract Major Capital¶

The funding landscape shows investors betting on vertical specialization over horizontal platforms. Healthcare AI attracted significant attention, with over $200M in funding during the period.

Hippocratic AI raised $141 million in Series B funding at a $1.64 billion valuation, bringing total funding to $278 million. The company launched a Healthcare AI Agent App Store with 300 AI agents covering 25 specialties. The safety network includes 6,000 nurses and 300 doctors providing oversight. Clinicians can create AI agent prototypes in 30 minutes and develop testable agents in 3-4 hours, with the company focusing on non-diagnostic clinical tasks to address healthcare staffing shortages.

Eleos secured $60 million in Series C funding for behavioral health AI agents, bringing total funding to over $120 million. The company provides an AI voice tool that runs in the background of therapy sessions to help clinicians analyze patient conversations, reducing administrative burden and improving documentation. Eleos expanded into 8 new states in 2024, now operating in 25 states total.

Auquan tripled revenue and closed an $8 million seed round for financial services AI agents. The company’s agents have helped top financial firms like MetLife and UBS eliminate over 50,000 hours of manual work, covering 550,000 entities and serving 25% of the top 25 private equity firms.

Ecosystem Watch: From Agents to Infrastructure¶

The ecosystem is expanding beyond individual agents to infrastructure for multi-agent systems and specialized applications across industries.

Infrastructure Vision¶

Cisco’s Outshift group proposed an “Internet of Agents”, a new network infrastructure layer designed to enable AI agents from different organizations to automatically collaborate. The vision requires industry-wide cooperation to create open standards, security frameworks, and reference implementations for distributed agentic computing. Cisco published a research paper titled “DAWN: Designing Distributed Agents in a Worldwide Network” in November 2024, and the company’s research found that 78.1% of companies plan to develop and put their own AI agent into production, while 51.1% already have an agent in production.

PwC and Microsoft announced a strategic collaboration to bring AI agents to clients, combining PwC’s industry expertise with Microsoft’s agent platforms. The partnership aims to accelerate enterprise adoption by providing proven agent implementations across business functions.

Market Sentiment¶

The AI agent crypto sector experienced a sharp correction, losing 43.5% of its market value over a weekend—from a $20 billion peak to $11.3 billion. Major tokens like VIRTUAL, AI16Z, and FARTCOIN saw significant losses. Despite the downturn, analysts project potential long-term growth, with bull case estimates reaching up to $250 billion. The volatility highlights the speculative nature of crypto-based AI agent projects compared to enterprise-focused implementations.

Vertical Deployments¶

Specialized AI agents are entering production across diverse industries:

  • Element5 launched Neos for healthcare prior authorization, automating eligibility verifications and authorizations in post-acute care
  • Spot AI introduced the Remote Security Agent, described as the industry’s first remote Video AI Agent for physical security, addressing $94.5 billion in annual retail theft losses
  • Linq unveiled an AI agent for live events that automates lead capture from badges and business cards, reducing manual follow-up from days to minutes
  • Oracle announced AI agents for Fusion Cloud Sales that help sales teams automate administrative tasks, with Gartner predicting these technologies will reduce prospecting and meeting prep time by over 50% by 2026
  • SOCi reported that its Genius AI solutions now account for over 10% of its annual recurring revenue within eight months of launch, surpassing $100 million in ARR
  • Iterate AI uses Amazon MemoryDB to accelerate its workforce management conversational AI agent, demonstrating infrastructure optimization for agent performance

Dharmesh Shah’s Agent.xyz platform surpassed 250,000 users , reflecting growing end-user adoption. AI voice agents emerged as a key trend, with Andreessen Horowitz publishing a comprehensive update on the state of voice-based AI agents and their applications in customer service and sales.

Context-aware agents are becoming a focus area, with researchers and companies exploring agents that can understand and adapt to changing context during conversations—something Goose’s open-source framework specifically addresses by allowing mid-task redirection.

The infrastructure for multi-agent systems is being built. From Cisco’s Internet of Agents vision to specialized vertical applications, the ecosystem is moving beyond isolated agents to interconnected systems that can collaborate across organizational boundaries.

Looking Ahead: The Agentic Transition¶

January 2025 marked a transition point in the evolution of AI agents. OpenAI’s Operator launch validated agentic AI as the next major platform shift beyond chatbots. The platform announcements from ServiceNow, NVIDIA, Cohere, and others demonstrate this isn’t just research—it’s product.

Enterprise surveys reveal both enthusiasm and practical barriers. The 93% adoption figure from eMarketer and 77% investment readiness from UiPath show strong momentum, but the 80% citing data integration challenges and BCG’s finding that only 25% of executives report meaningful value underscore the implementation gap.

Real-world deployments are proving value with concrete metrics: Baca Systems’ response time reduction from 5-7 minutes to 5-10 seconds, Fujitsu’s 67% productivity boost, Boost.ai and Sage’s 50-day deployment. These aren’t demos—they’re production systems delivering measurable business results.

Research advances address core reliability issues. AgentRefine’s self-correction framework, MIT’s indoor training effect, CACTUS’s domain-specific reasoning, Agent Laboratory’s automated research processes, and NIST’s security evaluations all tackle the barriers between experimental agents and production-ready systems.

Funding concentrates on vertical applications, with healthcare attracting over $200M through Hippocratic AI and Eleos. Investors are betting that specialized agents solving specific industry problems will beat general-purpose platforms, at least in the near term.

The “Internet of Agents” vision from Cisco suggests where this could go: a network infrastructure layer enabling agents from different organizations to collaborate automatically. It’s an ambitious vision requiring industry-wide cooperation, but it acknowledges that the future of agentic AI isn’t just individual agents—it’s multi-agent systems working together.

Industry experts predict 2025 as the breakout year. OpenAI’s product chief Kevin Weil says humanity is on the verge of AI agents that can complete real-world tasks. Sam Altman predicts agents will enter the workforce in 2025. Gartner forecasts that by 2028, at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by agentic AI, up from 0% in 2024.

The pieces are moving into place: models, platforms, evaluation tools, specialized applications, security infrastructure. The challenge now is execution at scale. The gap between demo and production remains, but the direction is clear: we’re moving from chatbots to agents, from tools to abilities.

As Vijoy Pandey, senior vice president at Cisco’s Outshift incubator, put it: “The rapid emergence of foundation model-based AI agents is driving us toward ensembles of multi-agent applications working together across the internet.” The verge is here.
