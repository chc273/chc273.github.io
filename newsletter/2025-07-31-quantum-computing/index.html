Three major error correction milestones emerged this month: a record-low error rate, the first magic state distillation on logical qubits, and extended coherence times. These technical achievements, combined with significant funding announcements and new deployments, show the field advancing on multiple fronts.

This comprehensive review covers 158 articles published between July 1 and July 31, 2025, surveying the latest developments in quantum computing. Error rates reached new lows, magic state distillation moved from theory to practice, and the first quantum computer began operating in space.

A Record Month for Error Correction¶

Researchers at the University of Oxford achieved a single-qubit gate error rate of 0.000015%—equivalent to one error per 6.7 million operations. This improves on the previous record of one error per 1 million operations set by the same team in 2014, representing a nearly sevenfold improvement. The breakthrough uses trapped ions controlled by microwaves at room temperature, reducing the infrastructure needed for error correction.

However, two-qubit gate error rates remain significantly higher at approximately 1 in 2,000, highlighting the ongoing challenge of extending single-qubit performance to multi-qubit operations. The research, published in APS Physical Review Letters, demonstrates that the error correction overhead required for fault tolerance can be substantially reduced.

QuEra, in collaboration with researchers from Harvard University and MIT, demonstrated the first experimental magic state distillation on logical qubits using their Gemini neutral-atom quantum computer. Magic state distillation, a concept proposed more than 20 years ago, is essential for enabling non-Clifford gates—operations required for universal quantum computing but that cannot be implemented directly with error-corrected Clifford gates alone.

The team’s protocol distills five imperfect magic states into one cleaner state with higher fidelity, all within the error-corrected logical layer using distance-3 and distance-5 logical qubit bundles. The work was published in Nature. As QuEra Chief Commercial Officer Yuval Boger noted , this demonstration proves that fault-tolerant magic state distillation is possible within logical qubits—a critical step toward scalable quantum computing.

Separately, physicists at Aalto University in Finland set a new record for transmon qubit coherence time, achieving 1 millisecond in maximum echo coherence time, with a median of 0.5 milliseconds. This surpasses the previous benchmark of 0.6 milliseconds. The longer coherence window extends the time available for error-free quantum operations, reducing the resources required for quantum error correction. The findings were published in Nature Communications.

These three advances—error rates, error correction processes, and coherence times—represent complementary progress on the fundamental challenge of building reliable quantum computers. Rather than a single breakthrough, July’s results show incremental but meaningful improvements across multiple technical dimensions.

Research Highlights¶

Quantum Computing Goes to Space¶

For the first time, a photonic quantum computer has been launched into space . The device, developed by an international research team led by the University of Vienna, is now operational on a satellite at 550 kilometers altitude in low Earth orbit. The compact 3-liter system consumes just 10 watts of power and is designed to withstand the rigors of launch and operate autonomously in the space environment.

Project lead Philip Walther explained that the mission will collect data on how quantum computing functions in space while also aiming to improve existing space technologies. The satellite launched on June 23, 2025, and represents a milestone in quantum technology deployment, potentially enabling more efficient satellite-based computing and new physics experiments.

Bell Test Certification and Teleportation¶

A global team of physicists from Leiden University, Tsinghua University, and Zhejiang University successfully used Bell’s test to certify genuine quantum behavior in a superconducting quantum processor with 73 qubits. They also confirmed genuine multipartite Bell correlations in systems of up to 24 qubits, with measurements deviating from classical limits by 48 standard deviations.

In another communication breakthrough, researchers at Northwestern University achieved quantum teleportation through over 30 kilometers of fiber optic cable carrying live internet traffic at 400 Gbps. This demonstrates that quantum communication can coexist with classical data streams on existing infrastructure, removing a major barrier to building a practical quantum internet.

Photonic Advances¶

Xanadu demonstrated a photonic qubit that can detect and correct its own errors at room temperature using a Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) state on a silicon chip. The breakthrough, published in Nature, could enable the production of error-correcting quantum states using conventional chip manufacturing tools.

A European research initiative called QLASS is developing a photonic quantum computer using glass chips with 200 reconfigurable optical modes. The project aims to build an operational device by 2026, with initial applications focused on designing better lithium-ion batteries.

Researchers at Harvard SEAS developed ultra-thin metasurfaces that can generate entangled photon states and perform quantum operations, effectively miniaturizing entire optical setups into a single device. The work, published in Science, was funded by the Air Force Office of Scientific Research and aims to overcome scalability challenges in quantum photonics.

Researchers from the Weizmann Institute and Quantum Source demonstrated deterministic entanglement generation using a single atom as an intermediary, achieving process fidelities of 99.6% for SWAP gates and 99.8% for CZ gates. This atom-mediated approach eliminates reliance on probabilistic photon generation, addressing a major bottleneck for scaling photonic quantum computers.

Simulation and Algorithms¶

Researchers used a digital trapped-ion quantum computer to study thermalization dynamics in a Z2 lattice gauge theory, employing randomized-measurement protocols to observe universal early-time signatures of quantum chaos. The system used 12 qubits across 15 trapped ions, demonstrating that current quantum computers can probe universal features of thermalization in complex many-body systems.

An international team simulated spontaneous symmetry breaking at zero temperature using a 7-qubit superconducting quantum processor, achieving over 80% fidelity. The system evolved from a classical antiferromagnetic state to a ferromagnetic quantum state, revealing quantum entanglement and correlations.

A study published in Nature Physics by researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Fudan University, and the University of Maryland demonstrated that quantum entanglement can accelerate quantum simulation algorithms. The team established a tighter error bound related to entanglement entropy and developed an adaptive algorithm, challenging the assumption that highly entangled systems are harder to simulate.

Quantinuum demonstrated a full set of fault-tolerant quantum gates using its trapped-ion quantum computer, marking a significant step toward scalable quantum systems that can withstand errors.

Industry Deployments and Products¶

Amazon Braket launched IQM’s 54-qubit Emerald superconducting quantum processor. The device features a square lattice architecture with 99.93% median single-qubit gate fidelity and 99.5% median two-qubit gate fidelity. Available in the Europe (Stockholm) Region, the system provides 19 hours of daily availability and is designed for surface-code error correction research.

Rigetti Computing achieved 99.5% median two-qubit gate fidelity on its modular 36-qubit system, representing a 2x reduction in error rate compared to its previous 84-qubit Ankaa-3 system. The milestone uses four 9-qubit chiplets and is a step toward scaling to 100+ qubit systems by the end of 2025.

D-Wave reported progress on its Advantage2 QPU, showing doubled coherence time and a 40% increase in energy scale. The company demonstrated quantum computational supremacy on a real-world magnetic materials simulation, solving it in minutes versus nearly one million years for classical methods—though this would require more electricity than the world’s annual consumption.

Quantum Computing Inc. (QCi) secured a $332,000 purchase order from a Top 5 U.S. bank for its quantum communication system, marking the company’s first U.S. commercial sale of quantum cybersecurity solutions. The system will form the core of a secure quantum security testbed to explore technologies like quantum key distribution and quantum authentication.

Strategic Partnerships¶

Denmark’s Magne System¶

EIFO and the Novo Nordisk Foundation are investing €80 million to establish QuNorth, a Nordic quantum initiative that will acquire and operate Magne—a 50-logical-qubit quantum computer built by Microsoft and Atom Computing. The Level 2 system, scheduled for operation by the end of 2026, will use 1,225 physical neutral-atom qubits and integrate with Microsoft’s Azure quantum software.

Danish Minister of Industry, Business and Financial Affairs Morten Bødskov called the investment “a decisive step in strengthening Denmark’s position.” The system aims to provide Nordic researchers and companies with early access to error-corrected quantum computing for applications in health, sustainability, and finance.

Moderna-IBM mRNA Collaboration¶

Moderna and IBM have demonstrated a hybrid quantum-classical approach for predicting mRNA secondary structures, a key step in designing mRNA medicines. Their research used Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) to improve Variational Quantum Algorithms and set a record in 2024 with a simulation involving up to 80 qubits for sequences of 60 nucleotides.

The collaboration aims to build a quantum-enabled biotech pipeline to augment classical computing for specific bottlenecks in drug discovery, as detailed in their published case study.

Additional Partnerships¶

Riverlane has integrated its Deltaflow 2 quantum error correction technology with an OQC quantum computer at a commercial data center, marking the first dedicated QEC deployment in a UK commercial setting. The system is part of the UK’s DECIDE project and aligns with national goals to achieve one million error-corrected operations by 2028.

Xanadu and HyperLight advanced thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) photonic chips, demonstrating waveguide losses below 2 dB/m and an electro-optic switch loss of about 20 mdB. The work was conducted in a high-volume semiconductor production facility.

India’s Chief Minister Nara Chandrababu Naidu announced that India’s first indigenously built 8-qubit quantum computer will be deployed in Amaravati in November 2025, supported by QpiAI. The announcement comes as QpiAI’s 25-qubit INDUS computer is already commercially operational in Bengaluru.

QEDMA, a developer of quantum noise resilience solutions, raised $26 million in Series A funding with participation from IBM. The company’s software aims to enable quantum computations up to 1,000 times larger by reducing, mitigating, and correcting errors.

QpiAI, an Indian quantum computing startup, raised $32 million in a Series A round led by Avataar Ventures, with participation from India’s National Quantum Mission. The company has developed a 25-qubit superconducting quantum computer, QpiAI Indus, and plans to scale to 64 qubits by year-end and 1,000 qubits by 2028.

Real-World Applications¶

Drug Discovery and Medicine¶

Moderna and IBM’s collaboration on mRNA secondary structure prediction represents one of the most advanced quantum computing applications in biotechnology to date. Their hybrid approach uses quantum algorithms to search for stable mRNA structures more efficiently than classical methods alone.

A study published in Nature introduced a hybrid classical and quantum computing model for classifying low-grade and high-grade gliomas using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas. The model combines ensemble feature selection with a variational quantum classifier, achieving a classification accuracy of 0.74 and identifying key molecular markers like IDH1 and PTEN.

The Wellcome Sanger Institute launched the Q4Bio Challenge , deploying quantum computing to tackle genomic challenges in collaboration with quantum software companies.

A study demonstrated how quantum computing could be applied to endometriosis research, while another explored quantum technology’s role in human health and drug discovery.

Researchers developed what they call the world’s first quantum metabolic avatar—a digital twin of human metabolism powered by quantum algorithms.

Materials and Chemistry¶

Scientists in Australia developed a quantum machine learning technique called Quantum Kernel-Aligned Regressor (QKAR) that models electrical resistance inside semiconductor chips. Their method outperformed traditional machine learning models by 8.8% to 20.1% in optimizing chip design using a dataset of 159 gallium nitride high-electron-mobility transistors.

A Cornell University study introduced quantum computational sensing (QCS), a method that integrates quantum computing with quantum sensing. The approach uses quantum computers to process sensor signals before measurement, achieving up to 26 percentage points better accuracy in classifying magnetic fields and brainwave patterns.

Researchers from Cleveland Clinic and IBM developed a hybrid computing approach that combines quantum computers with supercomputers to predict molecule stability. The method uses IBM Quantum System One for quantum calculations and high-performance computing for error correction, simulating a hydrogen ring with 18 atoms.

Oak Ridge National Laboratory researchers are developing a “synthescope”—a modified scanning transmission electron microscope—to write with atoms and tailor materials for future quantum technologies.

Optimization and Infrastructure¶

SoftBank conducted a trial in Tokyo using quantum computing to optimize 5G RAN performance. The test used an Ising machine to recalibrate base station carrier-aggregation settings, resulting in a 10% increase in downlink speeds and up to 50% growth in data transmission capacity. The operator plans to expand this quantum-based optimization into broader network architecture.

D-Wave’s collaboration with NASA JPL aims to scale quantum computing for space applications.

Mitsui & Co. partnered with Quantinuum to explore quantum computing applications across business sectors.

An AWS study demonstrated simulating fluid mechanics using quantum computing on Amazon Braket with Haiqu and Quanscient.

Looking Ahead¶

The pattern in July’s developments shows error correction advancing on multiple fronts—rates, processes, and coherence times—but these remain incremental steps toward fault tolerance, not sudden breakthroughs. The industry is building toward practical systems like Denmark’s 50-logical-qubit Magne computer due in 2026, while simultaneously developing near-term applications in specific domains.

The first quantum computer in space opens new environment testing possibilities and could enable future space-based quantum networks. Global funding continues to flow across modalities, particularly to European photonic and neutral-atom approaches.

The field is maturing: more demonstrations of specific capabilities with measured technical details, fewer claims of general superiority. The distinction between demonstrated results and projected capabilities is increasingly clear in public communications. Error correction remains the primary technical focus, but commercial applications are emerging in drug discovery, semiconductor design, and telecommunications optimization.
